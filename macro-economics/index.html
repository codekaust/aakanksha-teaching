Introductory Macroeconomics

Class XII · presentation decks for the classroom

Chapter 1
Introduction to Macroeconomics

What macroeconomics studies and why it was born in the Great Depression; the four sectors; stocks and flows; the circular flow of income built up sector by sector, with leakages and injections.

Diagrams: the water tank · two-sector circular flow · four-sector circular flow
Chapter 2
National Income Accounting

Final and intermediate goods; domestic territory and normal residents; the eight aggregates and the three conversions between them; all three methods of estimation worked on one dataset; the item-treatment table; real versus nominal GDP and the deflator; GDP and welfare.

Diagrams: the three switches · the conversion chain · value added bar chart
Chapter 3
Money and Banking

Barter and its five drawbacks; the functions of money; M1 to M4 and high-powered money; commercial banks and credit creation worked round by round; the six functions of the RBI; the quantitative and qualitative instruments of monetary policy.

Diagrams: M1–M4 composition · the credit-creation cascade
Chapter 4
Determination of Income and Employment

Aggregate demand and supply; ex ante and ex post; the consumption and saving functions with APC, APS, MPC and MPS; equilibrium by both the AD = AS and S = I routes; the investment multiplier; excess and deficient demand and their correction; the paradox of thrift.

Diagrams: C and S functions stacked · the 45° cross · the multiplier · both gaps, measured vertically
Chapter 5
Government Budget and the Economy

Objectives of the budget; public goods and the free-rider problem; revenue and capital receipts and expenditure with the two tests; balanced, surplus and deficit budgets; the revenue, fiscal and primary deficits and their implications; the government expenditure and tax multipliers.

Diagrams: the budget tree
Chapter 6
Open Economy Macroeconomics

The balance of payments and its two accounts; balance of trade versus current account balance; autonomous and accommodating transactions; the foreign exchange market and the determination of the exchange rate; depreciation, devaluation and the three exchange rate systems; the real exchange rate and purchasing power parity.

Diagrams: the foreign exchange market · a demand shift and its consequences
In class

→ or Space reveals the next step, then moves to the next slide · ← steps back · m opens the slide list · f goes full screen · Home / End jump to the first or last slide.

Every question appears as question → "are you ready?" → answer, so the class can attempt it before the answer is shown. Numericals reveal one line at a time. An ⓘ next to a phrase opens a panel explaining why it is worded that way — click only, never on the arrow keys.
