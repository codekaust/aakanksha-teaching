Chapter 1
Nature and Significance of Management

Every organisation you have ever been inside — your school, your father's shop, the hospital, the cricket team — is being run by somebody. This chapter gives that activity a name, a definition the examiner will accept, and a shape you will use for the next seven chapters.

Where we begin

You have been watching this all your life

There is no previous chapter to return to, so start with what you already know. Your school of 900 pupils decides a syllabus in April, hires two Physics teachers in May, draws a timetable in June, tells the teachers what to teach, and in September looks at the unit-test results and changes what is not working. Nobody in that building calls it "management". It is management.

A cricket captain setting a field, a family marrying off a daughter on a fixed budget, a dabbawala in Mumbai moving 200,000 lunchboxes a day with almost no errors — all of it is the same activity. This chapter only gives you the words.

What actually changes today

You already recognise management when you see it. What you cannot yet do is name it in the exam's words and prove it from the lines of a case. That, and not the idea itself, is what 80 marks are given for.

The route through this chapter

Eight sections. The last two — the functions and coordination — are the map of the entire first book.

Where the marks sit

Units 1, 2 and 3 together carry 16 marks of the 80-mark paper. This unit reliably supplies a 1-mark identification, a 3- or 4-mark "explain any three/four", and a case in which you must name a level of management or a function. Nothing in this chapter is optional.

How to size an answer — points = marks

Learn this now; it applies to all 80 marks

A Business Studies answer is never a paragraph. It is a numbered list of points, each with a bolded heading followed by one or two lines. And the number of points is set by the marks — which is why every question in this deck carries its mark weight in the tag:

  1. 1 markOne sentence. Usually a definition or a name. No heading needed.
  2. 3 marksThree headed points.
  3. 4 marksFour headed points.
  4. 5 or 6 marksFive or six headed points — and for a "distinguish between", a table with a named basis column.
Why the heading alone is worth something

Markers award roughly half the point for the correct term and half for the explanation. A student who writes four correct headings and no explanation still scores. A student who writes a beautiful unheaded paragraph often scores less than half, because the marker cannot find the terms.

Management — the definition, word by word

Definition · write it exactly like this

Management is the process of getting things done with the aim of achieving goals effectively and efficiently.

process

The primary functions a manager performs — planning, organising, staffing, directing, controlling. "Process" means it is a series, not a single act.

effectively

Doing the right task — finishing the activity and reaching the goal. Concerned with the end result.

efficiently

Doing the task correctly and at minimum cost — maximum output from minimum input. Concerned with the cost of resources.

The commonest one-mark loss in this chapter

"Management means getting work done from others." That sentence earns nothing. The three words process, effectively and efficiently are the mark scheme; drop one and you drop the mark.

Why must all three words appear?

Because each one rules out a different wrong answer. Process rules out the idea that management is one heroic decision — it is five functions repeated continuously. Effectively rules out the busy manager who never finishes anything. Efficiently rules out the manager who finishes everything by burning money.

A definition that says only "getting things done" describes a peon as much as a Managing Director. The three qualifiers are what make the sentence describe management and nothing else.

Effectiveness and efficiency, on one target

Definition

Effectiveness means doing the right task — completing the activity and achieving the goal within the given time, whatever the cost.

Definition

Efficiency means doing the task correctly and at minimum cost — obtaining the maximum output from the minimum input. A cost-benefit relationship.

Worked on a production target

Target: 5,00,000 bicycle chains this year, at a standard cost of ₹42 a chain.

  1. Effective, not efficientPower cuts force a second shift. All 5,00,000 are made — but overtime and diesel push cost per chain from ₹42 to ₹58. Goal reached, resources wasted.
  2. Efficient, not effectiveThe manager refuses the second shift and holds cost at ₹40 — but only 3,60,000 chains are made. The dealer's order is short, and a rival takes the customer. Cost controlled, goal missed.
  3. Both5,00,000 chains at ₹41 by re-sequencing the machines. This is what management means.
The examiner's line

Effectiveness and efficiency are two sides of the same coin — different, but interrelated, and management must balance them. Poor management is the result of both ineffectiveness and inefficiency.

The four quadrants — where a manager can end up

How to read this
  1. Read the horizontal axis first: right means cheaper.
  2. Then the vertical: up means the target was hit.
  3. The two amber boxes have one virtue and one failure each — that is where the exam sets its cases, and the case will describe exactly one of them.
  4. The heavy green box is the only quadrant management is aiming at; the rose box is neither.

Confusable pair 1 — effectiveness vs efficiency

Students routinely write…

"…the manager was very efficient because he completed the order on time." That is exactly backwards. Completing the order is effectiveness. Whether he did it without wasting money is efficiency. The word efficient in ordinary Hindi-English speech means "good at his job" — in this paper it means one specific thing: output per unit of input.

Four bases. Three would be enough for a 3-mark question — write the first three and keep "Test question" as the line you check your own answer against.
Basis Effectiveness Efficiency
Meaning Doing the right task — completing the activity and achieving the goal. Doing the task correctly, at minimum cost.
Concerned with The end result. The input-output ratio — resources consumed.
Cost Cost is not considered; the target must be met. Cost is the whole point; resources must be saved.
Test question "Was the goal achieved?" "At what cost was it achieved?"

Case — the Ludhiana chain line

Case 1 · Ekam Cycle Components, Ludhiana

Harjit Singh Bedi runs a 70-worker unit making bicycle chains for dealers in Punjab and Haryana. His annual contract is for 5,00,000 chains. Power failures cost him nine working days in July, so he ran a night shift for six weeks. The full 5,00,000 chains were delivered on the contract date, but the cost per chain rose from ₹42 to ₹58 because of overtime wages and generator diesel.

Question 1 3 marks

Identify whether Harjit's management was effective, efficient, both or neither. Give the concept, explain it in one line, and quote the line of the case that proves it.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. He was effectiveEffectiveness means doing the right task and achieving the goal — the entire contracted quantity reached the dealer on the promised date.
  2. He was not efficientEfficiency means minimum cost per unit of output — the cost per chain rose by ₹16, a 38% increase, for exactly the same output.
  3. VerdictEffective but not efficient — the top-left quadrant. Management requires both.
"The full 5,00,000 chains were delivered on the contract date, but the cost per chain rose from ₹42 to ₹58 because of overtime wages and generator diesel."

★ Challenge — the same firm, the other error

Case 2 · Ekam Cycle Components, the following year

Burnt by last year's costs, Harjit refuses all overtime and shuts the night shift. Cost per chain falls to ₹40, the lowest the unit has ever achieved, but only 3,60,000 of the contracted 5,00,000 chains are delivered and the Haryana dealer moves to a Rajkot supplier. At the review meeting his accountant says: "Sir, our costs are down 5% — this has been our best-managed year."

★ Challenge 4 marks

The accountant is wrong. Say why, using both concepts, and state what "well managed" would actually have required.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. He was efficientCost per chain fell to ₹40 — the best input-output ratio the unit has recorded. On this measure alone the accountant is right.
  2. He was not effectiveThe goal was 5,00,000 chains; 1,40,000 were never made. Effectiveness is concerned with the end result, and the end result was not achieved.
  3. Efficiency without effectiveness is worthlessGoods that do not reach the market cannot earn revenue — and here the customer was lost to a competitor permanently.
  4. What good management requiredAchieving the goal and controlling cost together — the two are two sides of the same coin and must be balanced, not traded off.
"Cost per chain falls to ₹40 … but only 3,60,000 of the contracted 5,00,000 chains are delivered and the Haryana dealer moves to a Rajkot supplier."

The seven characteristics — the shape of the answer

"Explain any five characteristics of management" is a 5-mark question that appears almost every year. Learn all seven; write the five you can explain best.

Hook Goal-oriented · Pervasive · Multidimensional · Continuous · Group activity · Dynamic · Intangible
A hook is not an answer

The hook gets the seven words back into your head in the exam hall. It earns nothing on its own — each one still needs its heading plus one explanatory line plus an example. The next four slides teach them that way.

Goal-oriented, and pervasive

Characteristic 1

Management is a goal-oriented process — every organisation exists for a set of basic goals, and management unites the efforts of different individuals towards achieving them.

Characteristic 2

Management is all-pervasive — it is required in all organisations (economic, social, political), of all sizes, and at all levels. Only the way it is applied differs, never the activity itself.

Case 3 · Vidya Niketan Public School, Jaipur

Principal Sunita Kaushik runs a 900-pupil CBSE school. Her stated goal for the session is to raise the Class XII average from 68% to 76%. She reorganises the timetable, appoints two Physics teachers and starts a Saturday doubt clinic. "Whether it is my school, the tyre factory across the road or the municipal dispensary," she tells a parent, "somebody has to decide what to do, get people to do it, and check whether it worked."

Question 2 3 marks

Two characteristics of management are visible here. Name them, explain each in one line, and quote the evidence for the second.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Management is a goal-oriented processThe school has a clearly stated goal — raising the Class XII average from 68% to 76% — and every step she takes unites the staff's efforts towards it.
  2. Management is all-pervasiveIt is required in every kind of organisation, of every size — a school, a factory and a dispensary alike.
  3. The proof of pervasivenessShe names three completely unrelated organisations and says the same three activities are needed in each.
"Whether it is my school, the tyre factory across the road or the municipal dispensary … somebody has to decide what to do, get people to do it, and check whether it worked."

Multidimensional — three things are being managed at once

Characteristic 3

Management is multidimensional — it is a complex activity with three dimensions: management of work, management of people and management of operations.

Management of work

Every organisation exists to do some work. A school manages education; a hospital manages treatment. The work is defined in terms of goals to be achieved.

Management of people

Two aspects: dealing with employees as individuals with different needs, and as a group. It means making their strengths effective and their weaknesses irrelevant.

Management of operations

The production process that turns input into output — whatever the organisation's basic activity is. It interlinks the management of work and of people.

The same three, in one school

Work: teaching a syllabus to 900 pupils by February. People: 46 teachers, one of whom is on maternity leave and two of whom will not share a staffroom. Operations: timetable, transport, examinations, results — the process that turns admitted children into Class XII pass certificates.

Continuous, group, dynamic, intangible

Characteristic 4

Management is a continuous process — the five functions are performed simultaneously and continuously by all managers all the time. It never ends and the functions are inseparable.

Characteristic 5

Management is a group activity — an organisation is a collection of diverse individuals with different needs, and management makes them grow and develop as a team towards a common goal.

Characteristic 6

Management is a dynamic function — it must adapt to the changing environment. An organisation interacts with its social, economic and political environment and has to change with it.

Characteristic 7

Management is an intangible force — it cannot be seen but its presence can be felt, in the way the organisation functions: orderly work, satisfied employees, targets met.

Dynamic, dated

McDonald's sells the McAloo Tikki and no beef in India — a menu changed for a market. Indian retailers who had never heard of a QR code were accepting UPI payments within two years of its 2016 launch. Adapting is not optional; it is a characteristic of the activity.

Case — three dimensions, seventy pairs of hands

Case 4 · Ekam Cycle Components, Ludhiana

Harjit Singh Bedi must ship 42,000 chains a month. He has 70 workers — one a diabetic fitter who cannot take the night shift, and two who have not spoken since a quarrel over a bonus. Between the two runs the line: wire drawing, heat treatment, riveting, plating, packing. "Miss any one of those three and the other two stop paying," he says. "And not one of the seventy can make a chain by himself — a chain leaves this shed only because seventy people move it along."

Question 3 3 marks

Two characteristics of management are visible here. Name them, explain each in one line, and quote the words that prove the second.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Management is multidimensionalAll three dimensions are named: management of work (42,000 chains a month), management of people (70 workers, one diabetic, two who will not speak) and management of operations (the five-stage line from wire drawing to packing).
  2. Management is a group activityAn organisation is a collection of diverse individuals with different needs, and management makes them grow and develop as a team towards a common goal — no single worker produces the output.
  3. The proof of the secondHe says the chain cannot be made by any one man and leaves the shed only because seventy people move it along — that is group effort producing a group result.
"Not one of the seventy can make a chain by himself — a chain leaves this shed only because seventy people move it along."

The reverse drill — build the case yourself

Now the other way round

Write a four-line case set in a family-run wedding catering business in Pune that could only be answered management is an intangible force — not dynamic, not continuous, not group activity.

Are you ready for a model answer? 🤔
Model case, and why it forces the reading
Model · Deshpande Caterers, Pune

Two 600-guest weddings ran on the same evening. At the Baner venue nobody could name who was in charge, yet the buffet opened at 8:00 sharp, no dish ran out and no staff member was idle. At the Kothrud venue the same number of staff and the same menu produced a queue at the dessert counter and two empty chafing dishes. "You cannot point at management in either hall," the bride's father said, "but you can taste the difference between the two."

  1. Which words force the reading"Cannot point at" gives you cannot be seen; "taste the difference" gives you its presence is felt. Together they are the definition of an intangible force.
  2. Why the other three are excludedNothing changes over time (so not dynamic), nothing is repeated across a period (so not continuous), and the case contrasts two outcomes, not the teamwork that produced them (so not group activity).
  3. The transferable trickA case that can only mean one thing always contains the defining phrase in disguised words. Find that phrase before you write anything.

★ Challenge — two characteristics in collision

Case 5 · Prayaas Software Services, Bengaluru

Nandita Rao heads a 300-engineer IT-services firm. When a large client switched from on-premise servers to cloud delivery in 2023, she retrained 90 engineers within seven months and rewrote the delivery model. "We did not stop doing anything we were already doing," she says. "Planning, hiring, reviewing — all of it carried on every single week while we rebuilt the practice around a new technology."

★ Challenge 4 marks

A student writes: "This shows management is a dynamic function." A second writes: "No — it shows management is continuous." Who is right? Justify, and quote the words that decide it.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Both are present — and the case says so deliberatelyThe examiner's trap is to make you choose. The correct answer names both and separates them.
  2. Dynamic functionManagement must adapt to the changing environment — the technological shift to cloud delivery forced a change of delivery model and the retraining of 90 engineers.
  3. Continuous processThe functions are performed continuously and simultaneously — planning, staffing and reviewing did not pause while the change was made.
  4. The deciding words"Rebuilt the practice around a new technology" is the dynamic half; "all of it carried on every single week" is the continuous half. One clause each.
"We did not stop doing anything we were already doing … all of it carried on every single week while we rebuilt the practice around a new technology."

Three sets of objectives, not one

Management does not serve only the owner. The examiner wants three sets, and wants you to know which benefit is credited to which set.

Set 1 · Organisational objectives

The economic objectives of the enterprise itself. Three of them:

  1. SurvivalEarning enough revenue to cover costs. The most basic objective — an organisation that cannot survive cannot pursue anything else.
  2. ProfitSurvival is not enough; profit is needed to cover costs and risks and to keep the business going.
  3. GrowthThe business must grow, measured by increase in sales volume, number of employees, number of products or capital investment.
The three, at one firm

Prayaas Software Services, Bengaluru: survival — renewing the two annuity contracts that pay the salary bill; profit — a 14% operating margin that funds the training budget; growth — 300 engineers today against 180 in 2021.

Social and personal objectives

Set 2 · Social objectives

Creating benefit for society. The three the board asks for:

  1. Environment-friendly methodsof production.
  2. Employment opportunitiesfor the disadvantaged sections of society.
  3. Basic amenities— schools, creches and health care for employees.
Set 3 · Personal objectives

What the employees want, which management must reconcile with organisational goals:

  1. Financial needscompetitive salaries and perks.
  2. Social needspeer recognition.
  3. Higher-level needspersonal growth and development.
The line students get wrong

A creche for employees' children is marked as a social objective, not a personal one — the NCERT lists "basic amenities like schools and creche" under social objectives. Personal objectives are about salary, recognition and growth. If you are unsure in the exam: social = owed to society outside, personal = owed to the people inside.

Case — one decision, three objectives

Case 6 · Ekam Cycle Components, Ludhiana

Harjit Bedi installs a ₹38 lakh effluent-treatment plant, adds a night-school shift so that 24 workers from the nearby resettlement colony can be hired without prior schooling, and raises the skilled-fitter wage by 11% after the union asked for parity with the Rajkot units. "The treatment plant is for the drain, the night school is for the colony, and the raise is for my own fitters — three different people are being served by three different decisions."

Question 4 3 marks

Identify the objective of management served by each of the three decisions.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Effluent-treatment plant — social objectiveUsing environment-friendly methods of production is a benefit created for society, not for the firm.
  2. Night-school shift — social objectiveProviding employment opportunities to the disadvantaged sections of society is the second listed social objective.
  3. The 11% wage rise — personal objectiveCompetitive salaries and perks for the firm's own employees is a personal objective, which management must reconcile with organisational goals.
"The treatment plant is for the drain, the night school is for the colony, and the raise is for my own fitters."

★ Challenge — whose objective is it really?

Case 7 · Deshpande Caterers, Pune

Ashwini Deshpande opens a creche at the kitchen unit so her 40 women staff can bring their children to work. Absenteeism falls from 14% to 4%, and she wins two more wedding contracts because the kitchen is now reliably staffed on Sundays. "I did it for them," she says, "but it has been the best business decision I ever made."

★ Challenge 4 marks

A student writes: "The creche is a personal objective, because it benefits the employees." Is that acceptable? Answer fully, and state what the safest exam answer is.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. The textbook classification is socialNCERT lists "basic amenities like schools and creche" under social objectives. Write that first — it is what the marking scheme carries.
  2. Why the student's reasoning is not stupidThe creche does serve employees, and personal objectives are about employees. The distinction is that personal objectives are the financial, social and growth needs of individuals — pay, recognition, development — not welfare amenities.
  3. An organisational objective is also being servedAbsenteeism fell by ten percentage points and two new contracts followed — that is profit and growth, which are organisational objectives.
  4. The real lessonThe three sets are not in conflict. Well managed, one decision serves all three — which is why the syllabus teaches them together.
"I did it for them, but it has been the best business decision I ever made."

Why management matters — five points

Importance of management · a 5-mark answer
  1. Management helps in achieving group goalsIt gives a common direction to individual effort, integrating people's aims into the organisation's goal.
  2. Management increases efficiencyBy reducing cost and increasing productivity through better planning, organising, directing and controlling.
  3. Management creates a dynamic organisationIt helps people adapt to change instead of resisting it, so the organisation survives a changing environment.
  4. Management helps in achieving personal objectivesThrough motivation and leadership, individuals develop team spirit and meet their own goals while meeting the organisation's.
  5. Management helps in the development of societyBy producing good quality goods, creating employment and adopting new technologies for the growth of the nation.
Notice the overlap — and use it

Points 4 and 5 restate the personal and social objectives from the last section, seen from the other side. That is not sloppiness; it is the same syllabus content answering two different question stems. Learn one list and you have most of the other.

Case — the school that had no management

Case 8 · Vidya Niketan Public School, Jaipur

Before Principal Kaushik arrived, the school had 46 teachers and no timetable committee. Three teachers taught the same chapter twice; the science lab was locked all of December because nobody had been made responsible for the key; four teachers resigned citing "no idea where this place is going". "There was no shortage of hard-working people here," she said at her first staff meeting. "There was a shortage of anyone pulling them in the same direction."

Question 5 3 marks

Which point in the importance of management is being illustrated? Name it, explain it, and quote the proof.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Management helps in achieving group goalsManagement arranges the factors of production and gives a common direction to individual effort so that the organisation's goal is reached.
  2. Why not "increases efficiency"Efficiency is about cost. The case never mentions cost — it mentions duplicated and wasted effort, which is a failure of direction, not of cost control.
  3. The proofThe staff were individually hard-working; what was missing was anything integrating individual goals with organisational goals.
"There was no shortage of hard-working people here. There was a shortage of anyone pulling them in the same direction."

★ Challenge — importance, or a characteristic?

Case 9 · Prayaas Software Services, Bengaluru

When GST replaced the old indirect-tax regime on 1 July 2017, Prayaas had to rewrite every client invoice format inside eleven days. Nandita Rao ran three evening workshops for the accounts team. "Half of them wanted to keep the old formats and argue with the client. My job that fortnight was not to invoice anybody — it was to get thirty people to stop resisting a change that was already law."

★ Challenge 4 marks

Is this the characteristic "management is dynamic" or the importance point "management creates a dynamic organisation"? Both use the same word. Decide, justify, and say how you tell the two question types apart in future.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. It is the importance pointManagement creates a dynamic organisation — it helps people adapt to change so that the organisation survives in a changed environment.
  2. Why not the characteristicThe characteristic "management is a dynamic function" is about management itself having to change. Here the manager is changing the attitude of thirty employees — the object of the sentence is the people, not the function.
  3. The proofHer stated job was to get people to stop resisting, which is precisely what this importance point describes.
  4. The test that transfersAsk who is doing the adapting. If management is adapting to the environment → characteristic. If management is making employees adapt → importance. Also date the fact: GST came into force on 1 July 2017.

Three questions, one method

"Is management an art?" "Is management a science?" "Is management a profession?" are three separate questions, and all three are answered the same way.

The method — use it for all three
  1. State the features of the categoryWhat makes something an art / a science / a profession? Three, three and five features respectively.
  2. Test management against each feature in turnOne line per feature, each with an example.
  3. Give a verdict, with the qualifierThe verdict is never a bare yes. It is "yes, and…", "yes, but not an exact one", or "not fully".
Students routinely write…

"Management is a science." Full stop. That loses the mark, because management deals with human behaviour and its principles cannot give identical results in every situation. Equally, "management is a profession" full stop loses the mark, because entry is not restricted. The qualifier is the mark.

Management as an ART

Definition

Art is the skilful and personal application of existing knowledge to achieve a desired result. It has three features.

  1. Existence of theoretical knowledgeEvery art has a systematised body of knowledge that its practitioners can learn — as music has ragas and painting has technique. Management has this: a large literature of principles and theories, and specialised institutes that teach it.
  2. Personalised applicationThe artist applies the same knowledge in his own individual style. Two singers trained in the same gharana do not sound alike. Management has this: two managers with the same MBA handle the same striking workforce completely differently.
  3. Based on practice and creativityAn artist improves through continuous practice and produces something new each time. Management has this: a manager gains from experience, and every situation demands a fresh combination of resources.
Verdict

All three features are satisfied, so management is an art. Nandita Rao and a rival Bengaluru CEO both studied the same delivery frameworks; one kept the client by retraining, the other lost it by hiring afresh. Same knowledge, different personal application — that is art.

Management as a SCIENCE

Definition

Science is a systematised body of knowledge that explains certain general truths or the operation of general laws. It has three features.

  1. Systematised body of knowledgeScience has its own concepts, theories and principles based on a cause-and-effect relationship. Management has this — an organised body of knowledge built up over more than a century.
  2. Principles based on experimentationScientific principles are arrived at by observation and repeated experiment. Management has this only in part: its principles were evolved by observation and testing, but the "laboratory" is a workplace full of human beings and the experiment cannot be perfectly repeated.
  3. Universal validityA scientific principle gives the same result everywhere. Management fails this: principles must be modified to suit the situation, because they deal with human behaviour, which changes.
Verdict — write the qualifier

Management is a science, but an inexact or behavioural science — not an exact science like physics or chemistry. Two parts of hydrogen and one of oxygen make water in Jaipur exactly as they do in Bengaluru; an incentive scheme that lifts output 20% in Ludhiana may lift it 2% in Pune.

Management as a PROFESSION — the honest verdict

Compare a doctor: no MBBS, no practice. There is no equivalent bar on a manager — that single row is what decides the answer.
Feature of a professionDoes management have it?
Well-defined body of knowledge Yes. A large, teachable body of management knowledge exists, taught at the IIMs and hundreds of other institutes.
Restricted entry through an examination or a degree No. Anybody may be called a manager without any management qualification. There is no statutory bar.
Professional association Yes. The All India Management Association (AIMA) exists — but it is not legally compulsory for a manager to be a member.
Ethical code of conduct Partly. AIMA has laid down a code of conduct — but it is not statutorily enforceable the way the ICAI's code is on a chartered accountant.
Service motive Yes. Management serves the owner by earning profit and serves society — but its primary aim remains the organisation's objectives.
What the board wants written

Management does not fully qualify as a profession. It satisfies the body of knowledge, the association, the code and the service motive, but it fails restricted entry and its code is not statutorily enforceable — so the correct answer is a qualified no, and the qualification must be stated.

Confusable trio — art vs science vs profession

Students routinely write…

"Management is an art, not a science" — or the reverse. Both are wrong. Management is both a science and an art: science supplies the knowledge, art supplies its personalised application. The two are not alternatives; every practising manager uses both every day. Only the profession question gets a negative answer.

Four bases. Note that the verdict row is where the marks are.
Basis As an art As a science
Nature of knowledge Knowledge is applied personally, in the manager's own style. Knowledge is a systematised body of principles and theories.
Derived from Practice and creativity — improves with experience. Experimentation and observation — cause and effect.
Result Result varies from manager to manager; no two applications alike. Result should be the same everywhere — and here management falls short.
Verdict Management is an art — all three features are met. Management is an inexact / behavioural science — universal validity fails.

Case — the family caterer who never studied management

Case 10 · Deshpande Caterers, Pune

Ashwini Deshpande left school after Class X and has run the family catering business for nineteen years. She has never attended a management course. Her costing method — worked out over the years and written in a notebook — is now copied by two rival caterers, and she was invited to speak at a Pune hoteliers' association meeting. "There is nothing stopping me calling myself the Managing Director tomorrow. Try telling the same thing to a woman who wants to call herself a doctor."

Question 6 4 marks

Ashwini's business is well run. Does that make her a professional manager? Answer with reference to the features of a profession, and quote the decisive line.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. She is a manager, and a good oneShe plans, organises, staffs, directs and controls — the definition of management makes no reference to a qualification.
  2. But management is not fully a professionThe deciding feature is restricted entry: a profession requires a qualifying examination or degree, and management does not.
  3. The features management does satisfyA well-defined body of knowledge, a professional association (AIMA) with an ethical code, and a service motive — her notebook costing method being copied is that body of knowledge in miniature.
  4. The verdictManagement is not fully a profession, so "professional manager" is a description of her conduct, not a legal status.
"There is nothing stopping me calling myself the Managing Director tomorrow. Try telling the same thing to a woman who wants to call herself a doctor."

★ Challenge — all three at once

★ Challenge 6 marks

"Management is a science, an art and a profession — all three, without qualification." Examine this statement. Six marks means six points: two on each of the three claims.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. As a science — the claim holdsManagement has a systematised body of knowledge whose principles were derived by observation and experimentation.
  2. As a science — the qualification the statement missesIts principles lack universal validity because they deal with human behaviour, so management is an inexact or behavioural science, not an exact one.
  3. As an art — the claim holdsThere is theoretical knowledge, it is applied in a personalised style, and it improves with practice and creativity.
  4. As an art — no qualification is neededAll three features of art are fully satisfied. This is the one part of the statement that is correct as written.
  5. As a profession — partlyManagement has a body of knowledge, an association (AIMA), an ethical code and a service motive.
  6. As a profession — where the statement failsEntry is not restricted and the code is not statutorily enforceable, so management does not fully qualify as a profession.

Conclusion to write at the end: the statement is correct for art, correct with a qualifier for science, and incorrect for profession.

The three levels of management

What each level actually does

Top level

Who: Chairman, Chief Executive Officer, Board of Directors, Managing Director, President, Vice-Presidents.

Does: responsible for the welfare and survival of the organisation; formulates overall goals and strategies; analyses the business environment and its implications; coordinates the activities of different departments; answerable to the shareholders.

Middle level

Who: divisional heads, plant superintendents, operations managers.

Does: the link between top and lower level; interprets the policies framed by top management; ensures the department has the necessary personnel; assigns duties and responsibilities to the level below; motivates them; and cooperates with other departments.

Supervisory level

Who: foremen, supervisors, superintendents — the lowest level of the hierarchy.

Does: directly oversees the efforts of the workforce; ensures the quality of output; minimises wastage of materials; maintains safety standards; and passes the workers' grievances upward.

The one-line test that decides every case

Top level formulates. Middle level interprets. Supervisory level executes. Find the verb in the case and the level names itself. The foreman is described as the lowest ranking manager and the highest ranking worker — he carries out plans, he does not make them.

Case — one decision, travelling down three levels

Case 11 · Ekam Cycle Components, Ludhiana

The Board decides that Ekam will enter the e-bike drivetrain market by 2027 and sanctions ₹4 crore for it. Rupinder Kaur, the plant superintendent, works out that this needs two new machining cells and a re-laid layout, and assigns the tooling changeover to her three shift heads. Balwinder, the foreman on B shift, stands at the cell every morning checking that the hardened sprockets are within tolerance and that scrap steel is under 2%.

Question 7 3 marks

Name the level of management at which each of the three people is working, and give the duty that identifies each one.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. The Board — top level managementIt formulates the overall goals and strategies of the organisation. Entering a new market and sanctioning ₹4 crore is a strategic decision, not an operating one.
  2. Rupinder Kaur — middle level managementA plant superintendent is the standard example. She interprets the Board's policy into a workable plan and assigns duties and responsibilities to the level below.
  3. Balwinder — supervisory (operational) levelA foreman directly oversees the workforce, ensuring the quality of output and minimising wastage of materials.
"Balwinder, the foreman on B shift, stands at the cell every morning checking that the hardened sprockets are within tolerance and that scrap steel is under 2%."

The reverse drill — write the level

Now the other way round

Write a three-line case set in the Jaipur school in which somebody is unmistakably doing middle level management — and could not be mistaken for either of the other two levels.

Are you ready for a model answer? 🤔
Model case, and why it forces the reading
Model · Vidya Niketan Public School, Jaipur

The management committee has ruled that no Class XII section may exceed 32 pupils. Alok Mathur, Head of the Science Department, works out that this means splitting one section and finding four extra periods a week. He tells the committee what staff he will need, and tells his six teachers which of them will take the new section and on which days.

  1. Which words force the readingHe does not make the rule — the committee did, so he is not top level. He does not teach the class himself under supervision, so he is not supervisory. He interprets a policy into a plan and assigns duties downwards — the two defining middle-level duties.
  2. The keyword the marker looks forHe is the link between the top and the operational level — he reports upward about staff and instructs downward about periods, in the same paragraph.
  3. The trick, generalisedTo pin the middle level, make the person receive a policy from above and hand duties down to other managers. A supervisor also takes instructions from above — but the people below him are workers, not managers. That is the line between the two lower levels, and the exam tests it constantly.

The five functions — the spine of this whole book

How to read this
  1. Start at Planning at the top and move clockwise.
  2. Each solid arrow means "this function supplies what the next one needs".
  3. The dashed amber arrow is the feedback loop: controlling returns its findings to planning, which is why the process never ends.
  4. Controlling is drawn amber because it is the box that closes the circle — Chapter 8 comes back to Chapter 4.

What each function is — one line each

  1. PlanningDeciding in advance what to do and how to do it. It means setting goals and drawing up the course of action to reach them. Planning is the bridge between where we are and where we want to be.
  2. OrganisingAssigning duties, grouping tasks, establishing authority and allocating resources so that the plan can actually be carried out.
  3. StaffingFinding and keeping the right people in the right jobs — recruitment, selection, training, appraisal. Also called people management or human resource management.
  4. DirectingInstructing, guiding, motivating and leading people while the work is going on. Its elements are supervision, motivation, leadership and communication.
  5. ControllingMeasuring actual performance against the standard and taking corrective action where there is a deviation — and feeding the result back into the next plan.
Students routinely swap the last two

Directing acts on people while the work is going on. Controlling acts on results after the work is done. "The manager told the team how to handle the client" is directing; "the manager compared the month's billing against target" is controlling.

Case — one wedding, five functions

Case 13 · Deshpande Caterers, Pune

Ashwini Deshpande takes a 600-guest wedding for 14 February. In November she fixes the menu, the ₹9.6 lakh budget and the delivery schedule. In December she splits the kitchen into a tandoor section, a sweets section and a service section, and puts one senior cook in charge of each. In January she hires eleven temporary staff and trains them for two evenings. On the day she walks the floor telling the service section to open the chaat counter early because the baraat is late. At midnight she totals the food actually consumed against the quantity indented, finds 8% wastage in the sweets section, and rewrites the indent formula for the next wedding.

Question 8 5 marks

Identify all five functions of management in this case, in the order they occur, quoting the act that shows each. Five marks means five points.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. PlanningDeciding in advance — the menu, the ₹9.6 lakh budget and the delivery schedule were fixed in November, three months ahead.
  2. OrganisingDividing the kitchen into three sections and putting one senior cook in charge of each is grouping tasks and establishing authority.
  3. StaffingHiring eleven temporary staff and training them is putting the right person in the right job.
  4. DirectingInstructing the service section on the day to open the chaat counter early is guiding people while the work is going on.
  5. ControllingComparing food consumed with food indented, finding 8% wastage and rewriting the indent formula is measuring performance against a standard and taking corrective action — and it feeds straight back into the next plan.
"At midnight she totals the food actually consumed against the quantity indented, finds 8% wastage in the sweets section, and rewrites the indent formula for the next wedding."

★ Challenge — where does one function end?

Case 14 · Vidya Niketan Public School, Jaipur

Principal Kaushik sets a target of a 76% Class XII average. In October the half-yearly results come in at 69%. She calls the four subject heads, shows them the subject-wise gap, tells them plainly that Chemistry is the weakest and that she expects a recovery plan by Monday, and then sits with the Chemistry head for an hour encouraging her not to take it as a personal failure.

★ Challenge 4 marks

One student says this is controlling. Another says it is directing. A third says planning. Settle it — and say what makes the boundary hard to see here.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Controlling is the main functionShe compares actual performance (69%) against the standard (76%), locates the deviation by subject and demands corrective action. That is the definition of controlling.
  2. Directing is also presentSitting with the Chemistry head and encouraging her is motivation, an element of directing — it acts on a person, not on a result.
  3. Planning is what happens next, not hereThe "recovery plan by Monday" has not been made yet. Control has triggered planning; it has not become it.
  4. Why the boundary is hardThe five functions are performed simultaneously and are inseparable — that is characteristic 4. In a case, name the dominant function first, then any secondary one, and justify each from a different line.
"She calls the four subject heads, shows them the subject-wise gap, tells them plainly that Chemistry is the weakest and that she expects a recovery plan by Monday."

Coordination — the essence of management

Definition · exam wording

Coordination is the process by which a manager synchronises and integrates the activities of different departments so that there is unity of action in the pursuit of common objectives.

Why it is the ESSENCE and not a sixth function

Coordination is not something a manager does after the other five functions. It is implicit and inherent in every one of them — planning coordinates ends with means, organising coordinates duties with authority, staffing coordinates people with jobs, directing coordinates effort with instruction, controlling coordinates results with standards. Like the thread in a garland, it runs through them all.

The classic Indian example

Mumbai's dabbawalas move roughly 200,000 lunchboxes a day between homes and offices with almost no errors, using a colour-and-code system and four separate handovers per box. No single dabbawala plans the network; what makes it work is the synchronising of collection, sorting, transport and delivery — coordination, not effort.

Why is coordination not counted as a sixth function?

Because a function is a separable activity with its own time and its own output — you can point at the week the plan was made or the day the staff were hired. You cannot point at "the coordination hour". Every one of the five functions is itself an exercise in coordination, which is why the NCERT calls it the essence rather than a function.

If a question asks "how many functions of management are there?", the answer is five. If it asks "is coordination a separate function?", the answer is no — it is the essence of management. Both are one-mark questions and both are commonly lost.

Coordination drawn as the thread, not as a box

The six features of coordination

  1. Coordination integrates group effortsIt unifies unrelated or diverse interests into purposeful work activity, giving a common focus to group effort.
  2. Coordination ensures unity of actionIt is the binding force between departments, ensuring all action aims at the organisation's goals — production must match what sales can sell.
  3. Coordination is a continuous processIt is not a one-time function: it begins at the planning stage and continues till controlling.
  4. Coordination is an all-pervasive functionIt is required at all levels and across all departments, because their activities are interdependent.
  5. Coordination is the responsibility of all managersTop, middle and supervisory levels all coordinate — it is not a specialist's job.
  6. Coordination is a deliberate functionA manager must synchronise efforts consciously and deliberately. Even willing cooperation needs to be given a direction.
Six features, so a six-mark question

"Explain the features of coordination" is a standard 5- or 6-mark question. Six headings, one explanatory line each, and — if the paper is a case — one example per feature drawn from the case.

Why coordination is needed — three reasons

Importance of coordination
  1. Growth in sizeas an organisation grows, the number of people increases and each differs in habits, background and approach. Their individual goals must be harmonised with organisational goals.
  2. Functional differentiationfunctions are split into departments, divisions and sections, each with its own objectives and style of working — and their objectives can conflict. Coordination links them back to the common goal.
  3. Specialisationmodern organisations employ specialists who believe only they can judge matters in their own field and will not take advice. Coordination by an independent person reconciles their differences.
Case 15 · Prayaas Software Services, Bengaluru

Sales offers a 15% discount to close a large annuity contract; Finance refuses, because the margin falls below the 12% floor the Board set. Delivery has already blocked twenty engineers for the account. Each of the three heads is doing exactly what her own department's objective tells her to do, and the deal is dying between them. Nandita Rao calls the three into one room and rebuilds the offer as a three-year term at 9% discount.

Question 9 3 marks

Which of the three reasons for coordination is at work here? Name it, explain it in one line, and quote the line of the case that proves it.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Functional differentiationThe work has been split into departments each with its own objective — Sales is measured on closing the contract, Finance on protecting the margin, Delivery on staffing the account — and those objectives conflict.
  2. Nobody in the case is at faultEach head is pursuing her own department's goal correctly. That is exactly the situation the textbook says coordination exists for: departmental goals must be tied back to the common organisational goal.
  3. What Nandita actually doesShe does not do Sales' job or Finance's job — she synchronises and integrates the three, restoring unity of action. Coordination is a deliberate act and the responsibility of every manager.
"Each of the three heads is doing exactly what her own department's objective tells her to do, and the deal is dying between them."

Confusable pair 2 — coordination vs cooperation

Students routinely write…

"There was good coordination in the team because everybody helped each other willingly." That sentence describes cooperation, not coordination. Willingness is an attitude and it is voluntary. Coordination is something a manager does deliberately — you can order coordination into existence; you cannot order goodwill into existence.

Four bases. The last row is the one that wins the mark.
Basis Coordination Cooperation
Meaning Orderly synchronising and integrating of the efforts of different departments to secure unity of action. Voluntary and willing effort of individuals to help one another at work.
Nature Deliberate and formal — a managerial function. Voluntary and informal — arises from people's attitudes.
Origin Arises from the authority of the manager. Arises from the goodwill of the employees; it cannot be ordered.
If the other is missing Coordination without cooperation leads to dissatisfaction among employees. Cooperation without coordination leads to wasted effort.

Case — willing hands, wasted effort

Case 16 · Ekam Cycle Components, Ludhiana

The purchase department, keen to help, bought 30 tonnes of high-carbon steel at a good price in a falling market. The production department, equally keen, had already re-tooled for the e-bike sprocket, which needs alloy steel. Both departments worked overtime that fortnight and both were pleased with themselves; the 30 tonnes sat in the yard for eleven months and production ran short of alloy stock in week three. Nobody had told either department what the other was doing.

Question 10 4 marks

Was the problem a lack of cooperation or a lack of coordination? Name it, define it, explain the consequence, and quote the proof.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Cooperation was presentVoluntary, willing effort was not missing at all — both departments worked overtime and both were trying to help the firm.
  2. Coordination was missingNobody synchronised and integrated the two departments' activities, so there was no unity of action towards the common objective.
  3. The consequence is the textbook oneCooperation without coordination leads to wasted effort — ₹ tied up in unusable steel for eleven months and a stock-out of the steel actually needed.
  4. Whose job it wasCoordination is the responsibility of every manager and is a deliberate function; it does not happen because people are well-meaning.
"Both departments worked overtime that fortnight and both were pleased with themselves; the 30 tonnes sat in the yard for eleven months and production ran short of alloy stock in week three."

★ Challenge — is coordination a function?

★ Challenge 6 marks

"Coordination is the sixth function of management." Do you agree? Give six points, and make sure at least three of them are features of coordination used as evidence.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. No — coordination is the essence of managementIt is implicit and inherent in all functions, not a separate one performed alongside them.
  2. It is present inside planningTop management plans for the whole organisation, which means reconciling departmental plans with each other from the very start.
  3. It is present inside organising and staffingStructure is built, and staffed, so that duties and authority fit together.
  4. It is present inside directing and controllingDirecting synchronises effort with instruction; controlling reconciles actual results with planned ones.
  5. Feature evidence — all-pervasive and continuousCoordination is required at every level and in every department, and runs from the planning stage right through to controlling — no single function does that.
  6. Feature evidence — the responsibility of all managersA separate function could be assigned to a separate manager. Coordination cannot: it is the job of every manager, which is precisely why it is called the essence of management, like the thread in a garland.
