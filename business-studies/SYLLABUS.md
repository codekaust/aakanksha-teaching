`https://cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/CurriculumMain27/SecPart2/BusinessStudies_SecP2_2026-27.pdf`
and against the NCERT textbooks in this folder (`business-studies-part-1.pdf`,

Re-verify at the start of each academic session. The syllabus has been rationalised more than once
and older question banks still carry topics that have been dropped — and, as §3 below shows, one

### Question paper design — why case studies dominate these decks

| Typology | Marks | % |
|---|---|---|
| Remembering and Understanding | 32 | **40%** |
| **Applying** | 24 | **30%** |
| **Analysing, Evaluating and Creating** | 24 | **30%** |

**60% of the paper is application or analysis.** A student who can recite the fourteen principles
but cannot spot *Unity of Command* in a three-line story scores 40% at best. That single number is
the reason every concept in these decks arrives with a named firm, a named person and a concrete
decision attached.

## 2. Units and topics

Reproduced from the CBSE curriculum document, condensed to the topic line.

### Part A — Principles and Functions of Management (Units 1–8, 50 marks)

| Unit | Topics |
|---|---|
| **1 · Nature and Significance of Management** | Management — concept, objectives, importance. Effectiveness and efficiency. Management as science, art and profession. Levels of management. The functions — planning, organising, staffing, directing, controlling. Coordination — concept and importance. |
| **2 · Principles of Management** | Principles of management — concept and significance. **Fayol's** fourteen principles. **Taylor's** scientific management — **principles *and* techniques**. Comparison of the contributions of Fayol and Taylor. |
| **3 · Business Environment** | Business environment — concept and importance. Dimensions — economic, social, technological, political, legal. **Demonetisation — concept and features.** |
| **4 · Planning** | Planning — concept, importance, limitations. The planning process. **Single-use and standing plans.** Objectives, strategy, policy, procedure, method, rule, budget, programme. |
| **5 · Organising** | Organising — concept and importance. The organising process. Functional and divisional structure — advantages, disadvantages, suitability. Formal and informal organisation. Delegation — concept, elements, importance. Decentralisation — concept and importance; delegation vs decentralisation. |
| **6 · Staffing** | Staffing — concept and importance. **Staffing as a part of Human Resource Management.** The staffing process. Recruitment — sources, merits and demerits of internal and external. Selection — the process. Training and development — concept, importance, and the methods: on-the-job and off-the-job, **induction**, vestibule, apprenticeship, internship. |
| **7 · Directing** | Directing — concept and importance. Elements of directing. Motivation — concept, **Maslow's hierarchy of needs**, financial and non-financial incentives. Leadership — concept and styles: **authoritative, democratic, laissez faire**. Communication — concept, the elements of the process, formal and informal, barriers, and how to overcome them. |
| **8 · Controlling** | Controlling — concept and importance. **The relationship between planning and controlling.** The steps in the process of control. |

| Unit | Topics |
|---|---|
| **9 · Financial Management** | Concept, role and objectives. The three financial decisions — investment, financing, dividend — meaning and the factors affecting each. Financial planning — concept and importance. Capital structure — concept and the factors affecting it. Fixed and working capital — concept and the factors affecting their requirements. |
| **10 · Financial Markets** | Financial markets — concept. Money market — concept. Capital market and its types, primary and secondary. Capital market vs money market; primary vs secondary. ## 3. Where the syllabus and the textbook disagree

Three places. Each one is called out on the slide where it matters, not left for the student to
discover in the exam hall.

### 3.1 Financial Markets is examinable but is no longer in the book

NCERT rationalised the *Financial Markets* chapter **out of** *Business Studies Part 2*. The 2026-27
reprint in this folder contains only three chapters — Financial Management (9), Marketing (10) and
Consumer Protection (11).

The CBSE curriculum document for 2026-27 nevertheless still lists **Unit 10 · Financial Markets**,
with its own learning outcomes, inside the 15 marks it shares with Financial Management.

**Consequence for these decks.** Chapter 10 is numbered by CBSE unit and is built from the
curriculum document and from current market practice rather than from the textbook. Where a fact
has moved since the old NCERT chapter was written — most obviously India's move to a **T+1 rolling
settlement cycle** — the deck teaches the current practice, dates it, and says what the older
textbook said. `index.html` and `part-2.html` both carry a warning box about this.

### 3.2 Part 2 chapter numbers do not match CBSE unit numbers

| CBSE unit — used for the deck filenames | NCERT *Part 2*, 2026-27 reprint |
|---|---|
| 9 · Financial Management | Chapter 9 · Financial Management |
| **10 · Financial Markets** | — not in the book — |
| 11 · Marketing | Chapter **10** · Marketing |
| 12 · Consumer Protection | Chapter **11** · Consumer Protection |

Part A's numbering is unaffected: CBSE Units 1–8 are NCERT Chapters 1–8.

### 3.3 Consumer Protection is the 2019 Act

The **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** replaced the Consumer Protection Act, 1986. The curriculum
document names the 2019 Act explicitly and even cites the gazette notification. What changed that a
student must not get wrong:

- **Commissions, not Forums** — District Commission, State Commission, National Commission.
- The **Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)**, which the 1986 Act had no equivalent of.
- **Product liability**, **e-filing** and **mediation** are new.
- The **pecuniary limits** were revised again by the Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the
  District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) **Rules, 2021** — later
  than the Act itself. Teach the 2021 figures, put the year on the slide, and add a `.note`
  wherever the textbook or an older guide still shows the pre-2021 limits.

Anywhere an older question bank still expects the 1986 wording, the deck teaches both and says which
one goes in the paper.

## 4. Things dated on the slide, on purpose

The NCERT text is dated in places, and a stale fact reads as an error rather than as history.

- **Business Environment.** The 1991 liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation reforms are
  *history* and belong in the deck as history. Demonetisation is dated **8 November 2016**. GST,
  current FDI rules and any recent policy change carry their year.
- **Financial Markets** moves fastest. SEBI's powers, listing requirements and the trading procedure
  have all changed since the old chapter was written. Where a figure is illustrative rather than
  examinable, the slide says so.
- **Never present an undated statistic or a repealed provision as current fact.**
