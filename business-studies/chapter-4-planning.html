Chapter 3 showed an environment that will not hold still — economic, social, technological, political and legal forces that a firm does not control and cannot switch off. Planning is the manager's first answer to that: you cannot stop the change, but you can anticipate it and decide your response in advance.

Why this chapter is worth teaching slowly

Units 4 and 5 together carry 14 marks. Within Unit 4, the examiner's two favourite questions are the limitations (which students under-prepare because the textbook prints the importance first) and naming the type of plan in a three-line case. Both are drilled here.

Planning — the concept

Definition — learn this wording

Planning is deciding in advance what to do, how to do it, when to do it and who is to do it. It bridges the gap between where we are and where we want to reach.

It always involves two things

  1. Setting objectivesthe ends — what is to be achieved.
  2. Developing courses of actionthe means — how it will be achieved.

So planning is concerned with both ends and means. A slide that names only the ends has named half the definition.

And therefore

Planning necessarily involves decision making — choosing the best alternative from among several. Where there is only one possible course of action, there is nothing to plan.

This is why "planning is a mental exercise" is a feature and not a slogan.

Why "bridges the gap" is the phrase to remember

How to read this
  1. Read left to right: today's position, then the position wanted.
  2. The dashed line underneath is the gap — the distance to be covered.
  3. The amber arch is the plan; it rests on the two piers, ends and means.
  4. Remove either pier and the arch falls — a goal with no method, or a method with no goal.

The first case — read it, then answer

Case 1 · Biryani Bowl Kitchens, Hyderabad

Sneha Rathod runs a cloud-kitchen chain with 18 delivery-only kitchens across Hyderabad, turning over ₹9 crore in 2025-26. In April 2026 she called her four zone heads together. "By March 2028 we will be running 30 kitchens and doing ₹18 crore," she said, "and we will get there by opening only inside existing food courts, so that we never pay for a standalone rent again." Rentals, delivery-app commissions and chicken prices were all worked out for the two years ahead before the meeting closed.

Question 1 3 marks

Name the function of management Sneha is performing, and show from the case that it involves both the things that function always involves.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Planningshe is deciding in advance what to do and how to do it — the primary function of management.
  2. The ends — setting objectives"30 kitchens and ₹18 crore by March 2028" is a specific, measurable objective within a time period.
  3. The means — the course of action"only inside existing food courts" is the how. Both halves are present, so the definition is fully satisfied.
"By March 2028 we will be running 30 kitchens and doing ₹18 crore … and we will get there by opening only inside existing food courts."

Features of planning — the first four

Seven features. A 4-mark question asks for any four with an explanation each.

1 · Planning focuses on achieving objectives

Organisations exist for a purpose. Plans set out specific goals and the activities to reach them. Planning that does not contribute to a predetermined goal is meaningless.

2 · Planning is a primary function of management

It lays the base for every other function — organising, staffing, directing and controlling are all performed within the framework of the plan. This is called the primacy of planning.

3 · Planning is pervasive

It is required at all levels and in all departments — not the property of top management. What differs is the scope: top management plans for the firm, middle management for the department, supervisors plan the day.

4 · Planning is continuous

A plan is made for a period — a month, a quarter, a year. When the period ends a new plan is drawn on new conditions. Plan → implement → plan again: the planning cycle never stops while the firm is alive.

All four in one firm

At Biryani Bowl Kitchens, Hyderabad, Sneha Rathod's "30 kitchens by March 2028" is the goal every other plan serves (focuses on objectives); nobody was hired, no van leased and no shift supervised until that plan existed (primary function); her zone heads plan the quarter while her counter managers plan the shift (pervasive); and the whole plan is redrawn every April on that year's rents and chicken prices (continuous).

Do not fuse these two

Pervasive answers who plans — everyone, at every level. Continuous answers how often — endlessly, in cycles. Students routinely write "planning is pervasive because it goes on all the time", which is the definition of the other feature and earns nothing.

Features of planning — the last three

5 · Planning is futuristic

It is a forward-looking function based on forecasting. Sales forecasting is what a firm's annual production plan is built on.

6 · Planning involves decision making

It means choosing from among alternatives. If there is only one course open, no planning is needed — the need for planning arises only when alternatives exist.

7 · Planning is a mental exercise

It is thinking rather than doing: foresight, imagination and sound judgement. It requires logical and systematic thinking, not guesswork or wishful thinking.

All three in one line

Harpreet Singh Sandhu of Nanak Woollens Exports, Amritsar forecasts European winter demand in February (futuristic), weighs shipping by sea at ₹42/kg against air at ₹190/kg (decision making), and does all of it on paper in his office before a single shawl is woven (mental exercise).

Hook Objectives · Primary · Pervasive · Continuous · Futuristic · Decision · Mental

The hook recovers the list when the mind goes blank. It earns nothing on its own — every feature still has to be written out and explained in a line.

Devansh Arora assembles LED drivers for three lighting brands. In January he drew up a production plan for the year. In July the price of a Chinese driver IC doubled, so he scrapped the January plan and drew a fresh one for August to March on the new component prices. ★ Challenge 4 marks

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. The feature misunderstood — planning is continuousplans are made for a period; when the period or the premises change, a fresh plan is drawn. The planning cycle does not end with one plan.
  2. The feature he confused it with — planning is futuristiclooking a year ahead is futuristic; redrawing the plan when the year turns out differently is continuous. Being forward-looking does not make one plan permanent.

Importance of planning — the six benefits

Six, and the examiner asks for any three, four or five of them. Each one gets a case here, because "provides direction" written bare is worth half a mark.

Hook Direction · Uncertainty reduced · Waste reduced · Innovation · Decision making · Standards for control

1 · Planning provides direction

By stating in advance how work is to be done, planning tells each department what action to take and in which direction. Without it, employees pull in different directions and the goal is never reached.

Nanak Woollens: the export target of 40,000 shawls tells weaving, dyeing and packing exactly what each must produce.

2 · Planning reduces the risks of uncertainty

Planning lets a manager look ahead and anticipate change. Uncertain events cannot be eliminated, but they can be anticipated and a response prepared.

Backwater Bliss: Anitha books the June–August monsoon months for refits, because she has forecast that occupancy will collapse then anyway.

3 · Planning reduces overlapping and wasteful activities

Planning is the basis of coordinating the efforts of divisions, departments and individuals. Useless and duplicated activity is minimised, and inefficiency becomes easy to spot.

Vertex Electronics: before the 2026 plan, purchase and stores each kept their own component register; the plan left one, and 400 hours of double entry a year with it.

Importance of planning — the last three

4 · Planning promotes innovative ideas

Because planning is the first function, it is where new ideas can take the shape of concrete plans. It is the most challenging activity of management, and it guides all future action, so it leads to growth and prosperity.

Vertex Electronics: the 2026 plan is where Devansh's idea of assembling for his own brand first became a schedule and a budget instead of a conversation.

5 · Planning facilitates decision making

Planning makes the manager evaluate each alternative and select the most viable one. Because targets are set and future conditions predicted, decisions become rational instead of impulsive.

Nanak Woollens: sea freight versus air freight was decided on a forecast delivery date, not on the mood of the morning.

6 · Planning establishes standards for controlling

Planning sets the goals or standards against which actual performance is later measured. If there were no standards, deviations could not be found, so planning is a prerequisite for controlling.

Biryani Bowl: "₹18 crore by March 2028" becomes the yardstick every quarterly review is held against.

Forward link — Chapter 8

Benefit 6 is the one to underline. It is the hinge on which Chapter 8 turns: planning without controlling is meaningless, and controlling without planning is blind. Every mark for "relationship between planning and controlling" is earned on this sentence.

Limitations of planning — and why you must know these cold

The examiner's favourite unfair question

The textbook prints importance first and at length, so most students revise it and stop. The paper asks for the limitations just as often — and a 5- or 6-mark "Explain any five limitations of planning" is a standard question. There are six. Learn all six; you cannot pick five out of four.

Hook Rigidity · Dynamic environment · Creativity reduced · Cost · Time-consuming · No guarantee of success
The sentence to close the answer with

"Despite these limitations, planning is not a useless exercise — it is a tool to be used with caution. It provides a base for analysing future courses of action, but it is not a solution to all problems." Examiners reward the balancing line.

Limitations 1–3 — each with the case that shows it

1 · Planning leads to rigidity

A well-defined plan fixes goals and a time frame, and managers may not be free to change it. Following a pre-decided plan after circumstances have changed can go against the organisation's own interest.

Case

Nanak Woollens, Amritsar: the annual plan fixed 60% of output for Germany. When the euro fell in October, the export manager was told he could not divert stock to the strong UK market because "the plan is the plan" — ₹31 lakh of margin lost.

2 · Planning may not work in a dynamic environment

The environment is constantly changing — economic, political, legal, social. A firm must keep adapting, and planning cannot foresee everything.

Case

Backwater Bliss, Kochi: Anitha's 2026-27 plan assumed 70% monsoon-season occupancy. An unseasonal red alert closed backwater cruising for eleven days in July and the whole quarter's cash budget had to be rewritten.

3 · Planning reduces creativity

Planning is done by top management; the rest merely implement. Middle managers are neither allowed to deviate nor to act on their own, so initiative is lost and people think along the same lines.

Case

Vertex Electronics, Noida: a line supervisor's idea for a cheaper heat-sink was turned down because "the year's process plan is already approved by the director; just run it as written."

Limitations 4–6 — each with the case that shows it

4 · Planning involves huge costs

Formulating plans costs time and money — checking facts, scientific calculation, boardroom meetings, fees to professional experts, preliminary investigation. The cost may not justify the benefit.

Case

Biryani Bowl: Sneha paid a consultancy ₹7.5 lakh for a city-expansion study on a project whose first-year profit was projected at ₹6 lakh.

5 · Planning is a time-consuming process

Sometimes the plan takes so long to draw up that there is not much time left to implement it. The opportunity the plan was written for may already be gone.

Case

Nanak Woollens: the Milan trade-fair plan took five months to finalise, by which time the fair's stall bookings had closed.

6 · Planning does not guarantee success

A plan succeeds only if it is properly drawn up and implemented. Managers tend to rely on previously tried and tested plans; that complacency and false sense of security can itself cause failure.

Case

Vertex Electronics: the 2027 launch reused the 2024 plan word for word, because "it worked last time" — and it failed, because the competitor of 2024 was no longer the competitor of 2027.

Writing the six-mark answer

Question 4 6 marks

"Planning is not a useless exercise, but it is not a guarantee either." Explain any six limitations of planning.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Planning leads to rigidityonce goals and the time frame are fixed, managers may not be free to change course when circumstances change.
  2. Planning may not work in a dynamic environmentthe environment keeps changing, and no plan can foresee every economic, political or legal shift.
  3. Planning reduces creativityplans are made at the top and merely implemented below, so subordinates' initiative is lost.
  4. Planning involves huge costsexpert fees, meetings and preliminary investigation may cost more than the plan is worth.
  5. Planning is a time-consuming processthe plan may take so long that too little time is left for implementation.
  6. Planning does not guarantee successreusing a previously successful plan breeds a false sense of security and can itself cause failure.

6 marks → 6 points. Six headings, one line each — not three headings with fat paragraphs. Close with the balancing sentence: planning is a tool to be used with caution, not a solution to all problems.

The planning process — seven steps, and it is a loop

How to read this
  1. Start at the amber box top-left and run left to right along the top row.
  2. At step 4 the flow drops down and then runs right to left along the bottom row.
  3. The two amber boxes are the two decision points: setting the goal, and choosing the route.
  4. The dashed cyan arrow is feedback — step 7 returns to step 1, which is why planning is a continuous function.

Steps 1–4, followed through one firm

Biryani Bowl Kitchens, Hyderabad — the same expansion from Case 1, walked step by step.

  1. Setting objectives. Objectives are the ends the management seeks to achieve. They must be stated for the organisation and then for every department and unit, in specific, measurable terms. Biryani Bowl: 30 kitchens and ₹18 crore revenue by March 2028 — and, under it, a kitchen-operations target of 4% wastage and a marketing target of 22% repeat orders.
  2. Developing premises. Premises are the assumptions about the future on which the plan is built — forecasts, existing plans, past policy information. They must be the same for everyone planning, or the departments will plan against each other. Biryani Bowl assumed: food-court rent rising 8% a year, delivery-app commission steady at 22%, and chicken at ₹230/kg. Every zone head planned on those three numbers.
  3. Identifying alternative courses of action. There is always more than one way to reach the objective; all of them should be listed. The more important the project, the more alternatives should be generated and discussed. Three routes were listed: (a) food-court counters, (b) standalone rented kitchens, (c) franchising the brand to existing restaurants.
  4. Evaluating alternative courses. Weigh the positive and negative aspects of each in the light of the objective — feasibility, cost, risk and consequences. Franchising was fastest but risked the food; standalone kitchens cost ₹18 lakh each to fit out; food-court counters cost ₹6 lakh and carried a rent share.

Steps 5–7, and where planning hands over

  1. Selecting an alternative. This is the real point of decision making. The best plan is the most feasible, most profitable, with the least negative consequences. Where mathematics cannot settle it, the manager's experience, judgement and intuition decide — and sometimes a combination of alternatives is chosen rather than one. Biryani Bowl chose food-court counters, plus franchising in two towns outside Hyderabad — a combination, which is perfectly good planning.
  2. Implementing the plan. Putting the plan into action — and this is the step at which the other functions of management enter. More output needs more labour and more machines, so organising, staffing and directing all begin here. Six counter managers hired, three vans leased, the Kukatpally marination unit commissioned.
  3. Follow-up action. Checking whether the plan is being implemented and activities are on schedule. Monitoring is part of the planning process, not an afterthought. Month-end review: 7 counters opened against a target of 9, so the March 2028 objective is re-examined on fresh premises.
This is the handover point

Step 7 is where planning meets controlling. Follow-up finds the deviation; controlling in Chapter 8 measures it, analyses it and corrects it; and what it finds becomes the premises of the next plan. That is the dashed arrow on the diagram.

The step everyone drops

Students routinely write…

…"objectives, alternatives, evaluate, select, implement, follow-up" — six steps, with developing premises quietly missing. It is a full mark gone, and it is the single most commonly dropped step in this chapter.

The other frequent loss is writing step 5 as "selecting the best alternative" and stopping. The examiner wants the phrase the real point of decision making.

Definition — premises

Premises are the assumptions about the future on which a plan is based. They are the base material upon which plans are drawn — forecasts, existing plans, past information about policies — and all managers involved in planning must use the same assumptions.

Why "the same assumptions" is not a detail

At Vertex Electronics, Noida, purchase planned on a copper price of ₹780/kg while sales quoted customers on ₹640/kg. Both departments planned competently. The firm lost ₹19 lakh on the first quarter's orders, because the premises did not match.

Two questions, and eight answers

The rest of this chapter answers one question — what kind of plan is this? There are two ways of cutting it, and the paper uses both.

Definition

Single-use plan — a plan developed for a one-time event or project. Such a course of action is not likely to be repeated in future, i.e. it is for non-recurring situations. It includes the names of the people responsible for the work. Budgets, programmes and projects are single-use plans.

Definition

Standing plan — a plan used for activities that occur regularly over a period of time. It is developed once and modified from time to time, and it greatly enhances efficiency in routine decision making. Policies, procedures, methods and rules are standing plans.

One firm, both kinds, one afternoon

At Biryani Bowl, the opening of the Kukatpally marination unit — its schedule, its ₹41 lakh budget, the four managers named — is a single-use plan: it happens once. The rule that every chicken batch is temperature-logged before marination is a standing plan: it applies to every batch, for ever.

Both belong to operational planning

Single-use and standing plans are both part of the operational planning process — short-horizon plans that help achieve operational goals. Keep that phrase; it is what sets up the ruling on the next slide.

The taxonomy of plans — the tree that answers half the paper

How to read this
  1. Start at the amber root and ask one question: is it used once, or again and again?
  2. Left branch = used once. Three leaves: budget, programme, project.
  3. Right branch = used repeatedly. Four leaves: policy, procedure, method, rule.
  4. The dashed line means "does not belong to either branch" — objectives and strategy sit outside the split.
Some guides call objectives and strategy "standing plans" — which do I write?

The sources genuinely disagree, and you should know it before the exam rather than during it. NCERT is explicit: after listing single-use and standing plans it says "there are other types of plans which usually are not classified as single-use or standing plans. A strategy, for example, is part of strategic planning… Objectives are usually set by top management and serve as a guide for overall planning." So on the NCERT position, objectives and strategy are in neither box.

Several popular guides and older question banks nevertheless print them as standing plans, on the reasoning that an objective or a strategy is not discarded after one use.

What to write. Teach and write the NCERT position — neither. If a question forces a binary choice ("classify the following as single-use or standing"), the safe answer is standing, because they are certainly not one-time plans — and add the half-line "strictly, objectives and strategy belong to strategic planning and are classified as neither." That sentence protects the mark under either marking scheme.

Distinguish — single-use vs standing plans

Four bases is the right length for a 3–4 mark "distinguish between" question.
BasisSingle-use planStanding plan
Meaning Developed for a one-time event or project. Used for activities that occur regularly over a period of time.
Repetition For non-recurring situations — not likely to be repeated. For recurring situations — used again and again.

Two traps in this table

One. A budget is single-use even though a firm makes one every year — because this year's budget is written for this year alone and is not reused. Two. Never answer "distinguish" with two paragraphs. No basis column, no marks — however correct the content.

One firm, one product, eight plans — the ladder

The eight types are not eight unrelated words. They are levels of specificity in one decision. Here is a single launch at Vertex Electronics, Noida, from the top of the ladder to the bottom.

1 · Objectives, and 2 · Strategy

Definition

Objectives are the ends which the management seeks to achieve by its operations — the desired future position the organisation would like to reach. They are set by top management, serve as a guide for overall planning, and must be expressed in specific, measurable terms within a given time period.

Definition

A strategy is a comprehensive plan for accomplishing an organisation's objectives. It has three dimensions: (i) determining long-term objectives, (ii) adopting a particular course of action, and (iii) allocating the resources necessary to achieve the objective.

Case 8 · Nanak Woollens Exports, Amritsar

Harpreet Singh Sandhu's board minute of 12 May 2026 reads: "We will take export sales from ₹14 crore to ₹25 crore by March 2029. We will do it by moving out of bulk shawls into design-led merino stoles, selling to European department stores instead of importers, and by putting ₹4 crore of retained earnings into a new design studio and two looms."

Question 6 4 marks

The minute contains two types of plan. Name both, and show all three dimensions of the second one in the quoted words.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Objective"₹14 crore to ₹25 crore by March 2029" — an end stated in specific, measurable terms within a time period.
  2. Strategy — dimension 1, long-term objectivesthe three-year ₹25 crore target is itself the long-term objective the strategy is built around.
  3. Dimension 2 — a particular course of action"moving out of bulk shawls into design-led merino stoles, selling to department stores instead of importers".
  4. Dimension 3 — allocating resources"putting ₹4 crore of retained earnings into a new design studio and two looms".
"We will take export sales from ₹14 crore to ₹25 crore by March 2029 … by moving out of bulk shawls into design-led merino stoles … and by putting ₹4 crore of retained earnings into a new design studio and two looms."

A strategy is always formulated keeping the business environment in view — here, European retail buying habits. Say so; it is often worth the fourth mark.

3 · Policy

Definition

Policies are general statements that guide thinking and channelise energies towards a particular direction. They are guides to managerial action and decision making in the implementation of a strategy, and they define the broad parameters within which a manager may use discretion.

What a policy always leaves you

Room for judgement. A policy states the firm's general response to a kind of situation; the manager still interprets and applies it. That is precisely what a rule refuses to leave.

Major and minor policies

Major company policies are for everyone to know — customers, clients, competitors. Minor policies are for insiders and carry detail vital to employees. Every level and every department has policies.

Case 9 · Biryani Bowl Kitchens, Hyderabad

Sneha Rathod's operations manual says: "Where a customer complains about a delivery, the kitchen manager may refund, replace or offer a credit — whichever he judges will keep the customer." A second line says: "Chicken older than 24 hours is never used."

Question 7 3 marks

Identify the type of plan in each of the two lines, and state the one basis on which you separated them.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. First line — a policya general statement guiding thinking that defines the parameters within which the manager uses his discretion.
  2. Second line — a rulea specific statement of what must not be done, allowing no flexibility or discretion whatsoever.
  3. The basis — discretion"whichever he judges" is discretion granted; "never" is discretion removed. That single word decides the answer in almost every policy-versus-rule case.
"…the kitchen manager may refund, replace or offer a credit — whichever he judges will keep the customer."

4 · Procedure, 5 · Method, 6 · Rule

Definition

Procedures are the routine steps on how to carry out an activity, specified in chronological order. They detail the exact manner in which work is to be performed, are generally meant for insiders, and are the steps taken within a broad policy framework to enforce that policy.

Definition

A method provides the prescribed way or manner in which one step of a procedure has to be performed. Selecting a proper method saves time, money and effort and increases efficiency.

Definition

Rules are specific statements that tell what is to be done and what is not to be done. They allow no flexibility or discretion, and they are the simplest type of plan.

The relationship, in one line

A policy sets the direction · a procedure is the ordered sequence that carries it out · a method is how one step of that sequence is performed · a rule stands alone and simply forbids or commands. Procedure ⊃ method; a rule is not a step of anything.

Case 10 · Vertex Electronics, Noida — all three in one workshop

Devansh Arora pinned three notices over the goods-inward bench. Procedure: incoming components — unload → sample 5% → test on the bench → tag accepted lots green → enter in the stores register. Method: the testing at step 3 is done on an automatic LCR meter rather than by manual multimeter. Rule: anti-static wrist straps are worn on the assembly floor at all times.

Read the three together: the procedure is the whole sequence, the method is how one box in that sequence is ticked, and the rule belongs to no sequence at all — which is exactly what the table on the next slides tests.

7 · Programme, and 8 · Budget

Definition

A programme is a detailed statement about a project which outlines the objectives, policies, procedures, rules, tasks, the human and physical resources required and the budget needed to implement a course of action. It covers the entire gamut of activities down to the minutest detail.

Definition

A budget is a statement of expected results expressed in numerical terms. It quantifies future facts and figures, so actual figures can be compared with expected figures and corrective action taken.

The two things the examiner rewards here

One. A programme is the broadest operational plan — it contains policies, procedures, rules and a budget inside it. Look back at the ladder: the programme rung swallows the four rungs below it — budget, procedure, method and rule — and it carries the project's own policies with it too. Two. A budget is both a plan and a control device — making it requires forecasting, which is planning; using it later to compare actual against expected is budgetary control, and it returns in Chapter 8.

Case 11 · Backwater Bliss Houseboats, Kochi

Anitha Menon's file for the new Alappuzha jetty runs to 40 pages: the objective, the approvals to be obtained, the hiring of eleven staff, the dredging contract, the safety rules for boarding, and a ₹2.1 crore statement of expected costs and receipts month by month.

Question 8 3 marks

Name the type of plan the 40-page file is, name the type of plan the last item in it is, and state the relationship between the two.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. The file is a programmea detailed statement about a project covering objectives, procedures, rules, tasks, human and physical resources and the budget — the entire gamut of activities.
  2. The last item is a budgeta statement of expected results expressed in numerical terms: ₹2.1 crore of costs and receipts, month by month.
  3. The relationshipthe budget is contained within the programme. Both are single-use plans, because the jetty is built once.
"…the safety rules for boarding, and a ₹2.1 crore statement of expected costs and receipts month by month."

★ Challenge — the sentence that could be two things

Case 12 · Biryani Bowl Kitchens, Hyderabad

Sneha Rathod's new counter manager is handed a single sheet. It says: "Vegetarian orders are packed on a separate table, in green-lidded boxes, before any non-vegetarian order is packed." Beside it, another sheet says: "This kitchen serves every community in Hyderabad; nothing we do should make a customer uncomfortable about the food."

★ Challenge 4 marks

A student says the first sheet is a rule. Another says it is a procedure. Decide, justify, and then name what the second sheet is and how the two are related.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. The first sheet is a procedureit prescribes steps in chronological order — separate table, green-lidded boxes, before the non-vegetarian packing. The word "before" is the giveaway: a sequence is being specified.
  2. Why it is not a rulea rule is a single specific statement of what must or must not be done, with no steps and no order — "no smoking in the kitchen" is a rule. This sheet has an order in it, so it is a procedure.
  3. The second sheet is a policya general statement that guides thinking and channelises energy in a direction, leaving the manager discretion about how to honour it.
  4. The relationshipprocedures are the steps taken within a broad policy framework to enforce the policy. The policy says "no customer should be uncomfortable"; the procedure is how that is delivered in the packing bay.
"Vegetarian orders are packed on a separate table, in green-lidded boxes, before any non-vegetarian order is packed."

The trio that loses the most marks

Students routinely write…

…"the company's objective is to become the market leader by exporting to Europe" — which fuses the objective and the strategy into one sentence and earns the marks for neither. Or: "their policy is to increase sales by 20%", which is an objective wearing the word policy. Or, worst of all, "the strategy is that customers are always right", which is a policy called a strategy.

The paper does not care how well you understood the case if you attach the wrong noun to it. The noun is the mark.

The three-question test — apply it in this order
  1. Does it say WHAT, in numbers and by a date?→ Objective.
  2. Does it say HOW we will get there, and what resources go into it?→ Strategy.
  3. Does it guide someone's day-to-day judgement, without saying exactly what to do?→ Policy.

Distinguish — objective, strategy and policy

Four bases across three columns is a 4-mark answer. Name every basis.
Basis Objective Strategy Policy
Meaning The ends management seeks to achieve — the desired future position. A comprehensive plan for accomplishing those objectives. A general statement that guides thinking and channelises energies.

One line that ties them together

An objective is the destination · a strategy is the route chosen and the fuel bought · a policy is the standing instruction the driver follows on any road.

The trio that looks identical on a factory floor

Students routinely write…

…"a procedure is a rule that has more steps", or "a method is a short procedure". Both are wrong, and both are marked wrong even when the case has been read correctly.

The other habitual loss: writing "rule" for anything that sounds strict. A strict sequence is still a procedure; a strict technique is still a method. A rule has no steps and no technique — it is a bare permission or prohibition.

Procedure ⊃ method. A rule is not part of either.
Basis Procedure Method Rule
Meaning The routine steps for carrying out an activity, in chronological order. The prescribed way in which one step of a procedure is performed. A specific statement of what must or must not be done.

Discretion The order of steps is fixed; judgement may remain inside a step. The technique is fixed; it is chosen to save time, money and effort. None at all, and a breach usually carries a penalty.

  2. "We will get there by moving into design-led stoles, selling direct to department stores, and investing ₹4 crore of retained earnings in a studio and two looms."

Question 9 · sorting drill 8 × ½ mark

Name the type of plan in each line, and say which of the eight are single-use, which are standing, and which are neither.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Objectivean end in specific, measurable terms within a time period. Neither single-use nor standing.
  2. Strategyall three dimensions present — long-term objective, course of action, allocation of resources. Neither.
  3. Policya general statement guiding recruitment decisions: "wherever a suitable candidate exists" leaves the manager discretion over who qualifies. Standing.
  4. Procedureroutine steps in chronological order. Standing.
  5. Methodthe prescribed way of performing one step of that procedure. Standing.

The pattern to memorise from this answer: policy, procedure, method, rule are standing · programme, budget (and project) are single-use · objective and strategy are neither — and if a question forces a two-way choice, write standing and add the half-line "strictly, these belong to strategic planning and are classified as neither", which is safe under either marking scheme. Each of the eight lines can be settled by one word — by when (1), how and with what (2), wherever (3), → (4), not by (5), without (6), file (7), numbers (8). Note that (3) and (6) are the pair to watch: "wherever" grants discretion, so policy; "without" removes it, so rule.

⇄ Now the other way round — write a procedure

Now the other way round

Take one situation — a Kochi houseboat crew handling a guest who wants to extend the cruise by a day. Write, for that one situation, a policy, a procedure, a method and a rule. Four sentences. Each must be unmistakably its own type.

Are you ready for a model answer? 🤔
Model answer, and the words that force the reading
  1. Policy"A guest who wishes to extend a cruise should be accommodated wherever the boat is free." "Wherever… free" leaves the captain judgement — a general guide to thinking.
  2. Procedure"Extension request: captain phones the booking desk → desk checks the next booking → tariff is quoted → advance is collected → the log is amended." The arrows are the point: a chronological sequence.
  3. Method"The booking desk checks availability on the online chart, not by calling the other captains." One step of the sequence, done a prescribed way.
  4. Rule"No extension is granted after 6 p.m., because night navigation on the backwaters is prohibited." No discretion, and a breach is a breach.

Test your own four the same way: a policy must contain a judgement word, a procedure must contain an order, a method must name a technique for one step, and a rule must contain a never or always.

Planning and controlling — the loop you will close in Chapter 8

Forward link — Chapter 8

Planning is prescriptive and looks ahead; controlling is evaluative and looks back. Planning sets the standard; controlling measures actual performance against it, finds the deviation, and takes corrective action — and what it finds becomes the premises of the next plan.

Two sentences carry every mark on this relationship, and they are worth learning verbatim now: "Planning without controlling is meaningless, and controlling without planning is blind." The first because a plan nobody checks is a wish; the second because you cannot measure a deviation from a standard that was never set.

How to read this
  1. The solid arrow runs forward: planning hands controlling a standard to measure against.
  2. The dashed arrow runs backward: it is the feedback, not a second plan.
  3. Cut the dashed arrow and planning never learns — cut the solid one and controlling has nothing to measure.

Exam practice — three marks

Question 11 3 marks

"Planning is a mental exercise and it involves decision making." Explain these two features, and state a third feature that follows from the first two.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Planning is a mental exerciseit is an intellectual activity of thinking rather than doing, requiring foresight, imagination and sound judgement, and logical and systematic thinking rather than guesswork.
  2. Planning involves decision makingit means choosing the best from among alternative courses of action. If only one course were open, no planning would be needed — the need arises only when alternatives exist.
  3. Planning is futuristicbecause the choice is made by thinking ahead, planning is a forward-looking function based on forecasting.

Exam practice — six marks

Question 13 6 marks

Explain the steps in the planning process.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Setting objectivesobjectives are set for the organisation and then for every department and unit, in specific, measurable terms.
  2. Developing premisespremises are the assumptions about the future on which the plan rests; all planners must use the same ones.
  3. Identifying alternative courses of actionevery possible way of reaching the objective is listed; the more important the project, the more alternatives.
  4. Evaluating alternative coursesthe pros and cons of each are weighed for feasibility, cost and consequences in the light of the objective.
  5. Selecting an alternativethe real point of decision making — the most feasible and profitable course, sometimes a combination of courses.
  6. Implementing the plan, and following it upthe plan is put into action, drawing in the other functions of management; follow-up then checks that activities are on schedule, and what it finds feeds the next plan.

6 marks → 6 points. Steps 6 and 7 are merged here only because the question asked for six; if it asks for "the steps" with no number, write all seven separately. Never drop developing premises to make the count fit.

Full case study 1

Case 14 · Vertex Electronics, Noida

Devansh Arora decided in April 2026 to add a solar-inverter line. He fixed a target of ₹12 crore of inverter sales by March 2029 and assumed, on the basis of the government's rooftop-solar push, that residential demand would grow 20 per cent a year. His team listed four options — importing complete units, assembling from imported kits, manufacturing from components, or a joint venture — and compared them on capital cost, margin and delivery time. They chose kit assembly, and by August a 40-page file existed covering approvals, the hiring of 45 people, the training schedule, the vendor list, the safety rules for the new floor, and a ₹5.8 crore cost-and-revenue statement for the first year.

Question 14 6 marks

(a) Identify and explain the first four steps of the planning process visible here. (b) Name the type of plan the 40-page file is, and the type of plan the last item in it is, and classify both as single-use or standing.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Setting objectives"₹12 crore of inverter sales by March 2029" — an end stated in specific, measurable terms within a time period.
  2. Developing premises"residential demand would grow 20 per cent a year, on the basis of the rooftop-solar push" — an assumption about the future drawn from the political and economic environment.
  3. Identifying alternative courses of actionthe four options — import, kit assembly, full manufacture, joint venture.
  4. Evaluating alternative coursesthey were compared on capital cost, margin and delivery time — the pros and cons weighed against the objective.
  5. (b) The file is a programmea detailed statement about a project covering objectives, procedures, rules, tasks, human and physical resources and the budget. It is a single-use plan.
  6. (b) The last item is a budgetexpected results expressed in numerical terms — ₹5.8 crore of costs and revenue. Also a single-use plan, and it sits inside the programme.

Full case study 2 — the mixed one

Case 15 · Backwater Bliss Houseboats, Kochi

Anitha Menon's 2027 plan fixed the fleet at nine boats, set out a ₹6.4 crore statement of expected receipts and costs for the year, and laid down the sequence to be followed for every booking — enquiry → check the availability chart → quote the tariff → collect a 30% advance → enter it in the boat log. In March, a cruise company offered her a 40-boat charter contract that would have doubled turnover. Her general manager turned it down without referring it upward, because the plan said "nine boats", and by the time Anitha heard of it the contract was gone. At the year-end review her finance head said, "The plan was excellent — we hit every number in it."

Question 15 6 marks

(a) Identify and explain two limitations of planning visible in this case. (b) Name the type of plan the booking sequence is and the type the ₹6.4 crore statement is, and classify each as single-use or standing. (c) Comment on the finance head's sentence.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Planning leads to rigiditythe plan fixed the fleet at nine boats and the manager was not free to depart from it even when departing was obviously in the firm's interest.
  2. Planning reduces creativitythe general manager did not even refer the opportunity upward — subordinates neither deviate from plans nor act on their own, so initiative is lost.
  3. (b) The booking sequence is a procedureroutine steps in chronological order, followed afresh for every booking — a standing plan. (Had the case said how one of those steps is performed — "availability is checked on the online chart, not by phone" — that one line would be a method.)
  4. (b) The ₹6.4 crore statement is a budgetexpected results expressed in numerical terms for the year — a single-use plan, because next year's is drawn up afresh.
  5. (c) The finance head is measuring the wrong thingthe plan was met, and the firm still lost. This is "planning does not guarantee success" — a plan faithfully executed can still be the wrong plan.
  6. (c) And it shows why controlling is not enough on its owncontrolling compares actual with planned; it cannot tell you the standard itself was too small. That judgement belongs back in planning, on fresh premises.
