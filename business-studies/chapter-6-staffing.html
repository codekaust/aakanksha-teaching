Staffing — the definition to reproduce

Definition

Staffing is the managerial function of filling and keeping filled the positions in the organisation structure. It is achieved by first identifying the requirement of the work force, followed by recruitment, selection, placement, training, appraisal, promotion and development of personnel.

Read the definition twice

"Filling" — finding a person for an empty post. "and keeping filled" — the person who retires, resigns or is promoted leaves a hole behind, so staffing never finishes. In a new enterprise staffing follows planning and organising; in an existing enterprise it is a continuous process.

Where the mark is lost

Students write "staffing means appointing employees" and stop. That is one-third of the definition. The examiner is scanning for "filling and keeping filled the positions in the organisation structure" — the phrase that links staffing back to organising.

Three features that decide the answer

1 · A function of every manager

Not only of the HR department. The plant head who picks his own shift in-charge is performing the staffing function.

2 · Both a line and a staff activity

A line duty of every manager, and the advisory work of the Human Resource Department. This exact phrase is examinable.

3 · A continuous process

Posts fall vacant through retirement, resignation, promotion and transfer. Staffing runs as long as the organisation does.

Example · Aarogya Care Hospitals, Bengaluru

The HR department designs the nurse-recruitment policy (staff role). But the head of the ICU decides which of the three shortlisted nurses actually joins her unit (line role). Both are staffing.

Why the examiner cares

"Staffing is both a line and a staff activity" is a stock 3-mark answer. It is also the sentence that stops a student from writing that staffing is only the HR department's job.

Why staffing matters — five benefits

This is 5-mark territory, and the five points are almost fixed. Learn them as five headings.

  1. Filling positions with competent peopleit helps in discovering and obtaining competent personnel for the various jobs shown in the organisation structure.
  2. Higher performanceperformance rises by putting the right person on the right job — the same worker is productive in one post and useless in another.
  3. Continuous survival and growthsuccession planning for managers ensures that the enterprise does not collapse when a key person leaves or retires.
  4. Optimum utilisation of human resourcesby avoiding over-manning it prevents under-utilisation of staff and high labour cost; by warning of shortages in advance it prevents disruption of work.
  5. Job satisfaction and moraleobjective appraisal and fair reward for contribution improve satisfaction and morale, and reduce employee turnover.
Why staffing has become more important

Three reasons the book gives, worth a line each: rapid advancement of technology, the increasing size of organisations, and the complicated behaviour of human beings.

What poor staffing actually costs

Case 2 · Dihing Valley Tea Estate, Guwahati

Bikram Saikia took over a 900-hectare tea estate in 2025. To save money he stopped hiring trained pluckers and put whoever turned up at the gate straight onto the bushes. Within one season the fine-leaf count fell from 68% to 41%, three workers were injured on the pruning machines, and the estate's second-flush price fell by ₹42 a kilo.

Question 2 4 marks

Identify the managerial function Bikram Saikia has neglected, and state three consequences of neglecting it that the case illustrates.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer

4 marks → 4 points. Naming the function is itself a point — so one heading for the function and three for the consequences.

  1. The function neglected is Staffinghe has not filled the positions with the right kind of people in the right number at the right time.
  2. Lower productivity and poor qualityuntrained pluckers dropped the fine-leaf count from 68% to 41%, so the produce itself is inferior.
  3. Wastage of material, time and effortleaf plucked wrongly cannot be re-plucked; a season's growth is lost.
  4. The enterprise cannot sell profitablythe second-flush realisation fell ₹42 a kilo — poor quality reaches the market as a lower price.
"the fine-leaf count fell from 68% to 41%, three workers were injured on the pruning machines, and the estate's second-flush price fell by ₹42 a kilo."

The injuries also point forward: this is exactly the gap that training is supposed to close, and we reach it later in this chapter.

Staffing as a part of Human Resource Management

Definition

Human Resource Management is the specialised branch of management that carries out the staffing function in a large organisation. As an enterprise grows and the number of persons employed increases, a separate department with specialists in managing people is created.

How to read this
  1. Read the two top boxes as two different people doing the same function.
  2. Blue on the left is the line manager; violet on the right is the specialist.
  3. Both arrows end in the same amber box — neither alone is staffing.
  4. Amber always marks the node the diagram is about.

The specialised duties HRM performs

A standing 5–6 mark question: "Human resource management includes many specialised activities and duties." Explain.

  1. Recruitmentsearching for qualified people and stimulating them to apply.
  2. Analysing jobscollecting information about jobs so as to prepare job descriptions.
  3. Developing compensation and incentive plansdeciding what each job is worth and how performance will be rewarded.
  4. Training and developmentfor efficient performance today and career growth tomorrow.
  5. Maintaining labour and union–management relationskeeping the relationship between workers and management workable.
  6. Handling grievances and complaintsgiving employees a route by which a complaint is heard and settled.
  7. Social security and welfareproviding for the safety, health and welfare of employees.
  8. Defending the company in law suitsand avoiding legal complications in the first place.
Answer strategy

Eight duties, and a 6-mark question wants six. Write the six you can explain, each with its heading. Never list all eight as bare words with no explanation — headings alone earn about half.

How HRM grew out of labour welfare

The evolution — one line each

The trade union movement created the need for a link between owners and workers → the labour welfare officer. The factory system put thousands under one roof → hiring became one man's job → the personnel officer, then the personnel manager. The human relations approach plus fast technological change made people a resource that can be developed → the human resource manager.

The point of the story

The name changed three times because the view of the worker changed three times — from a person to be pacified, to a post to be filled, to an asset to be developed. Staffing is an inherent part of human resource management: it is the practice of finding, evaluating and establishing a working relationship with people, for a purpose.

Do not fuse these two

Students write that "HRM replaced staffing". It did not. HRM is staffing performed by specialists. Staffing remains a duty of every manager; the HR department only supplies expert, advisory support.

The staffing process — eight steps

How to read this
  1. Start at the numbered 1, top left, and read the top row left to right.
  2. The thick arrow drops you to the second row, which runs right to left.
  3. The dashed cyan arrow is the feedback loop: it returns to step 1, because promotion, retirement and resignation create fresh vacancies.
  4. Blue = acquiring the person; green = keeping and growing the person.

Step 1 · Estimating the manpower requirements

Definition

Estimating manpower requirements means finding out how many people are needed and of what type. It is done through workload analysis — an assessment of the number and types of human resources necessary to perform the various jobs — and workforce analysis — the number and type available.

Understaffed

Workload > workforce. Start the recruitment process.

Optimally staffed

Workload = workforce. The desirable position.

Overstaffed

Workforce > workload. Requires transfer elsewhere or removal of employees.

Example · Awadh Fresh Supermarkets, Lucknow

Opening four new stores in 2026 needs 4 store managers, 12 floor supervisors and 60 sales assistants (workload analysis). The group already has 3 supervisors idle at its closed Kanpur outlet (workforce analysis). So it recruits 4 + 9 + 60, not 4 + 12 + 60.

The output of step 1

Manpower requirements must then be translated into a specific job description and the desirable profile of its occupant — qualifications, experience, personality characteristics. That document is what steps 2 and 3 search against.

Steps 2 and 3 · Recruitment and Selection, in one line each

Step 2 · Recruitment

The process of searching for prospective employees and stimulating them to apply for jobs in the organisation. Its essential objective is to create a pool of prospective candidates.

Step 3 · Selection

The process of choosing from among the pool of prospective candidates developed at the stage of recruitment, through a series of tests and interviews.

Example · Awadh Fresh Supermarkets, Lucknow

For four store-manager posts the group advertised in Dainik Jagran and 214 people applied — that whole activity is step 2. Reading those 214 forms and calling 30 of them for a test is the first act of step 3. Notice that step 2 ends the moment the last application arrives.

Coming up

These two steps carry the most marks in the chapter, so each gets a full section of its own later — recruitment with its sources, selection with its eight-step funnel — and a third section on nothing but telling the two apart.

Step 4 · Placement and orientation

Placement

Placement refers to the employee occupying the position or post for which he has been selected. It is the act of putting him in the chair.

Orientation / Induction

Orientation is introducing the selected employee to the other employees and familiarising him with the rules and policies of the organisation — a brief presentation about the company, an introduction to superiors, subordinates and colleagues, and a walk around the workplace.

Students routinely fuse these two

"Placement and orientation are the same thing — joining the company." They are not. Placement is the post; orientation is the people, the rules and the policies. A candidate can be placed on Monday and oriented over the whole of that week.

Example · Aarogya Care Hospitals, Bengaluru

A new radiographer is placed in the CT unit of the Whitefield hospital. On her first morning she is oriented: shown the infection-control protocol, introduced to the duty radiologist and the two technicians on her shift, and walked through the emergency exits.

Why the book calls this "crucial"

The employee's experience during orientation and placement forms his first impression of the organisation, and has a lasting impact on his decision to stay and on his job performance.

Steps 5, 6 · Training and development, and performance appraisal

Step 5 · Training and development

What people seek is not simply a job but a career. Organisations run in-house training centres or ally with training institutes to ensure continuing learning. The organisation gains too: competencies are strengthened, people perform better, and the firm is able to attract and retain talented people.

Step 6 · Performance appraisal

Performance appraisal means evaluating an employee's current and past performance against certain predetermined standards. The employee is expected to know what the standards are, and the superior is to provide feedback. The process therefore has three parts: defining the job, appraising performance, providing feedback.

Forward to Chapter 8

Notice the shape of appraisal: a standard is set, actual performance is measured, the deviation is identified and acted on. That is controlling applied to one person — the subject of Chapter 8.

Example · Sanchit Speciality Chemicals, Vadodara

Standard: 2 batch rejections per 100. Actual for Rohit Vasa, shift chemist, in 2025-26: 7 per 100. The appraisal interview identifies the cause as unfamiliarity with the new reactor — so the outcome is not dismissal but training.

Steps 7, 8 · Promotion and career planning, and compensation

Step 7 · Promotion and career planning

Managers must design activities that serve employees' long-term interests and encourage them to realise their full potential. Promotions mean being placed in positions of increased responsibility, and usually bring more pay, more responsibility and more job satisfaction.

Step 8 · Compensation

Compensation refers to all forms of pay or rewards going to employees. It is of two kinds — direct financial payments (wages, salaries, incentives, commissions, bonuses) and indirect payments (employer-paid insurance, vacations).

Time-based plan

Salary or wages paid daily, weekly, monthly or annually. Rewards presence.

Performance-based plan

Paid according to piecework — for example per unit produced. Rewards output.

What else shapes a pay plan

Legal requirements (labour laws), the union, company policy and equity — the felt fairness of one person's pay against another's.

Recruitment — the definition, and one crucial word

Definition

Recruitment is the process of searching for prospective employees and stimulating them to apply for jobs in the organisation.

The word that decides the marks

Recruitment is a POSITIVE process. Its object is to create a large pool of prospective candidates — to attract, to widen, to invite. It never rejects anybody. Rejection is selection's job, and selection comes next.

The four activities involved

(a) Identification of the different sources of labour supply; (b) assessment of their validity; (c) choosing the most suitable source or sources; and (d) inviting applications from prospective candidates for the vacancies.

Case 6 · Dihing Valley Tea Estate, Guwahati

For the 2026 first flush Bikram Saikia's estate needed 300 extra pluckers within three weeks. It put up a notice at the estate gate, sent word through its two labour contractors, and ran an announcement on the local community radio — 1,140 people came forward. Only then did the field manager begin to test who could pluck two leaves and a bud cleanly.

Everything before the word "Only" is recruitment: the pool grew from 0 to 1,140. Everything after it is selection.

The two families of sources

How to read this
  1. Read downwards from the amber node: the tree divides once, then again.
  2. Blue = internal, from inside the firm. Violet = external, from outside.
  3. Only two internal sources exist — the pair is a stock 1-mark question.
  4. The ten violet leaves are a list to be named, not derived; learn them in this order.

Internal sources — transfer and promotion

Transfer

Shifting an employee from one job to another, one department to another or one shift to another, without a substantive change in responsibilities and status. It is a horizontal movement. It may change duties and working conditions, but not necessarily salary.

Promotion

Shifting an employee to a higher position carrying higher responsibilities, facilities, status and pay. It is a vertical movement, and it has a great psychological impact because a promotion at a higher level may set off a chain of promotions below it.

Case 7 · Dihing Valley Tea Estate, Guwahati

Bikram Saikia's Sonapur division has four factory clerks and only enough work for two. The Namrup division is short of two clerks. He moves two clerks from Sonapur to Namrup on the same grade and the same pay, and in the same month makes the senior weighing clerk the assistant factory in-charge on a higher grade.

Question 4 3 marks

Name the two internal sources of recruitment used here and state the difference between them.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Transferthe two clerks moved from Sonapur to Namrup on the same grade and the same pay — a horizontal shift with no substantive change in responsibility or status.
  2. Promotionthe weighing clerk became assistant factory in-charge on a higher grade — a vertical shift to higher responsibility, status and pay.
  3. Both are internal sourcesno one was brought in from outside; the surplus in one division filled the shortage in another.
"moves two clerks from Sonapur to Namrup on the same grade and the same pay, and in the same month makes the senior weighing clerk the assistant factory in-charge on a higher grade."

Merits of internal sources

Five points, and this is a standard 5-mark question. 5 marks → 5 points.

  1. Employees are motivated to improve performancea promotion at a higher level sets off a chain of promotions below it; employees work with commitment and loyalty, and peace prevails in the enterprise because promotional avenues exist.
  2. Selection and placement are simplifiedcandidates already working in the enterprise can be evaluated more accurately and economically — this is a more reliable way of recruiting because the candidate is already known.
  3. Transfer is a tool of trainingit prepares employees for higher jobs, and people recruited from within do not need induction training.
  4. It shifts workforce from surplus to shortagestaff can be moved from over-manned departments to those short of people, instead of hiring afresh.
  5. It is cheaperfilling jobs internally costs far less than advertising for and processing outside candidates.

Limitations of internal sources

  1. No fresh talentcomplete reliance on internal recruitment carries the danger of inbreeding by stopping the infusion of new blood into the organisation.
  2. Employees may become lethargicif they are sure of time-bound promotions, the incentive to perform disappears.
  3. A new enterprise cannot use it at alland no organisation, however old, can fill all its vacancies from within.
  4. The spirit of competition is hamperedemployees who never face an outside candidate stop comparing themselves against the market.
  5. Frequent transfers reduce productivitya person moved too often never becomes expert at anything.
The hidden limitation students miss

An internal source does not create a new employee. Promote the weighing clerk and the weighing clerk's post is now vacant. Internal recruitment moves the hole; it does not fill it. Somewhere, sooner or later, an external source must be used.

External sources — the ten, and how to remember them

Hook Direct · Casual · Advertisement · Exchange · Placement agencies · Campus · Recommendations · Labour contractors · Television · Web
A better way to hold them — three groups

They come to us: direct recruitment, casual callers. We announce: advertisement, advertising on television, web publishing. Someone supplies them to us: employment exchange, placement agencies and management consultants, campus recruitment, recommendations of employees, labour contractors.

Never let the hook replace the teaching

The exam does not ask you to list ten words. It asks: "which source should this firm use, and why?" The next two slides give each source the sentence that makes it identifiable in a case.

External sources · 1 to 5

  1. Direct recruitmenta notice is placed on the notice-board of the enterprise; job-seekers assemble outside the premises on the stated date and selection is done on the spot. Used for casual vacancies of unskilled or semi-skilled jobs. Such workers are called casual or 'badli' workers and are paid on a daily-wage basis. It is very inexpensive — there is no advertising cost.
  2. Casual callersreputed organisations keep a database of unsolicited applicants. The list is screened as vacancies arise. Its merit is that it reduces the cost of recruitment.
  3. Advertisementin newspapers or trade and professional journals; used when a wider choice is required, and most senior positions in industry and commerce are filled this way. More information about the job and the organisation can be given. Its disadvantage: it may bring a flood of responses from unsuitable candidates.
  4. Employment exchangerun by the Government; a good source for unskilled and skilled operative jobs. In some cases notification of vacancies to the exchange is compulsory by law. Weakness: the records are often not up to date and many candidates referred are unsuitable.
  5. Placement agencies and management consultantsthey compile bio-data of a large number of candidates and recommend suitable names to their clients, for a fee. Useful where extensive screening is required. Management consultancy firms specialise in middle-level and top-level executive placements.

External sources · 6 to 10

  1. Campus recruitmentcolleges and institutes of management and technology as a source for technical, professional and managerial jobs. Firms maintain a close liaison with universities, vocational schools and management institutes.
  2. Recommendations of employeesapplicants introduced by present employees or their friends and relatives. They are likely to be good employees because their background is sufficiently known, and a type of preliminary screening has already taken place — the employee knows both the company and the candidate.
  3. Labour contractorsthey keep close contact with labourers and can supply the required number of unskilled workers at short notice. The danger: if the contractor leaves, all the workers employed through him leave with him.
  4. Advertising on televisionvacant posts are telecast along with the detailed requirements of the job and the profile of the organisation.
  5. Web publishingwebsites dedicated to job-seekers and job openings, visited by both employers and candidates. Today this includes company career portals and professional networking sites; the NCERT text calls it web publishing, and that is the phrase to write.
Currency, 2026

Employee referrals and web publishing have grown enormously — by the mid-2010s several large Indian firms already reported that over 40% of their hires came through referrals (LinkedIn India Recruiting Trends, 2016). Quote such figures as illustrative, with the year; the examinable content is the list of sources and their merits, not the percentage.

Merits of external sources

Four points. 4 marks → 4 points.

  1. Qualified personnelthe management can attract qualified and trained people to apply for the vacant jobs.
  2. Wider choicewhen vacancies are advertised widely, a large number of applicants apply, so management has a wider choice while selecting.
  3. Fresh talentexternal recruitment brings new blood into the organisation, which internal sources by definition cannot.
  4. Competitive spiritexisting staff have to compete with outsiders, so they work harder to show better performance.
Example · Aarogya Care Hospitals, Bengaluru

Aarogya's new cardiac unit needed an interventional cardiologist in 2026. No internal candidate held the qualification, so a management consultant was engaged. That is qualified personnel and fresh talent in one move.

Limitations of external sources

  1. Dissatisfaction among existing staffthey may feel their chances of promotion are reduced, which causes frustration and can hurt morale.
  2. Lengthy processthe vacancy must be notified and the firm must wait for applications before the selection process can even begin.
  3. Costly processa great deal of money goes on advertisement and the processing of applications.
Reading the pair as one answer

Every merit of internal is a limitation of external, and the reverse. Internal is cheap, quick, motivating, but stale; external is fresh and wide, but slow, costly and demotivating to insiders. If you can say that sentence, you can answer any question on either.

Distinguish · Internal vs External sources

Four bases is the right length for a 4-mark "distinguish between" question.
Basis Internal sources External sources
Meaning Filling a vacancy from within the organisation, through transfer or promotion. Filling a vacancy from outside the organisation, through advertisement, campus recruitment, agencies and so on.
Fresh talent Brings no new blood; carries the danger of inbreeding. Brings fresh talent and new ideas into the organisation.
Cost and time Cheap and quick — no advertising, and the candidate is already known. Costly and lengthy — advertising, processing and waiting for applications.
Effect on existing staff Motivates them; promotional avenues keep peace in the enterprise. May cause dissatisfaction, as staff feel their promotion chances are reduced.
The fifth basis, if a 5-mark question asks for it

Suitability. Internal sources suit a case where the skill already exists inside the firm and a surplus department can supply the person. External sources suit a new enterprise, a specialised skill nobody inside holds, and any vacancy where a wider choice is wanted.

Now the other way round

Write a four-line case set in a Vadodara speciality-chemicals plant that could only be answered labour contractors — not direct recruitment, not employment exchange.

Are you ready for a model answer? 🤔
A model case, and why it forces the reading
Model

Sanchit Speciality Chemicals must empty and re-line two storage tanks during a nine-day shutdown beginning next Monday. It needs 80 unskilled men at three days' notice, and it has no list of its own; Mr Devraj Parmar, who is on Sanchit's own rolls, keeps contact with a floating pool of such workers across Padra taluka and undertakes to bring them. The plant will not employ any of them after the shutdown.

  1. "80 unskilled men at three days' notice"labour contractors are the source that can supply the required number of unskilled workers at short notice. This rules out employment exchange, whose records are slow and often out of date.
  2. "it has no list of its own"rules out casual callers, which requires a database of unsolicited applicants.
  3. "Mr Devraj Parmar, who is on Sanchit's own rolls … undertakes to bring them"this is the defining feature: labour contractors are themselves employees of the organisation who bring workers with them.
  4. Why not direct recruitmentdirect recruitment is a notice at the gate and workers assembling on their own; here a named intermediary brings them.

★ Challenge · Internal or external?

Case 9 · Sanchit Speciality Chemicals, Vadodara

Sanchit is opening a brand-new pharmaceutical-intermediates plant at Dahej — a technology it has never worked with. The board is split. Harish Modi, the Works Director, says: "Promote our own people. We must go outside."

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer

6 marks → 6 points. An evaluation question must end with a stated recommendation, or the last mark is not earned.

  1. What the Works Director is relying onthe merits of internal sources — it motivates employees, is economical, and the candidate is already known, which simplifies selection and placement.
  2. The limitation he is ignoringinternal sources bring no fresh talent — the danger of inbreeding. Nobody inside has run a hydrogenation line, so promoting cannot create the skill.
  3. What the Technical Director is relying onthe merits of external sources — qualified personnel, wider choice and fresh talent.
  4. The limitation he is ignoringexternal recruitment is lengthy and costly, and it causes dissatisfaction among existing staff whose promotion chances now look smaller.
  5. The deciding fact in the casethis is a technology the firm has never worked with. Where the skill does not exist inside, an internal source can only move the vacancy, not fill it.
  6. Recommendationuse both: recruit the hydrogenation specialists and the plant head externally, and fill safety, maintenance and administrative posts internally by transfer and promotion — so the safety culture travels to Dahej and existing staff are not demotivated.
"Promote our own people. They know our safety culture, it costs nothing, and the men we promote will be loyal."

Selection — the definition, and the opposite word

Definition

Selection is the process of identifying and choosing the best person out of a number of prospective candidates for a job. The candidates take a series of employment tests and interviews; at every stage many are eliminated and a few move on to the next, until the right type is found.

The word that decides the marks

Selection is a NEGATIVE process. Recruitment at Dihing Valley attracted 1,140 people for 300 vacancies; selection exists to reject the other 840. It eliminates, it narrows, it rejects. Write the word negative and the mark is secure.

Why the rigour is worth its cost — two reasons

(i) It ensures the organisation gets the best among those available. (ii) It enhances the self-esteem and prestige of those selected, and conveys to them the seriousness with which things are done in the organisation. A rigorous process is therefore also a message to the person who survives it.

A useful caution

A selection decision can go wrong in two directions: a reject error — turning away someone who would have performed well — and an accept error — hiring someone who then performs poorly. Both are costly, which is why the process has eight steps and not two.

The selection process — a funnel that narrows

How to read this
  1. Start at step 1 at the top and read downwards; each bar is narrower than the one above it.
  2. The rose lines on the right are exits: a candidate can be rejected at any of the first six steps.
  3. A candidate who leaves the funnel never re-enters it — there is no arrow going back in.
  4. Amber marks the end point: the contract of employment, where selection finishes.

Steps 1 and 2 · Preliminary screening, and the tests

Step 1 · Preliminary screening

Preliminary screening helps the manager eliminate unqualified or unfit job-seekers on the basis of the information supplied in the application forms. Preliminary interviews at this stage help reject misfits for reasons that did not appear in the application form.

Step 2 · Selection tests

An employment test is a mechanism — a paper-and-pencil test or an exercise — that attempts to measure certain characteristics of individuals.

TestWhat it measuresUse it in a case when…
Intelligence testLevel of intelligence quotient — the person's learning ability and ability to make decisions and judgments. the job needs reasoning and judgment.
Aptitude testThe individual's potential for learning new skills — the capacity to develop. a fresher will be trained after joining.
Personality testClues to a person's emotions, reactions, maturity and value system; probes the overall personality. the job needs temperament, honesty or calm.
Trade testThe existing skills already possessed — level of knowledge and proficiency in the trade. an experienced welder or nurse is being hired.
Interest testThe pattern of interests — what a person is drawn to. fitting a person to the right role.
 Five tests. In a case-based question the examiner names the need and expects you to name the test.

The pair the examiner loves · aptitude vs trade test

Students routinely write…

"An aptitude test checks whether he can do the job." That answer fits either test and therefore earns neither mark. Aptitude = potential to acquire a skill. Trade test = the actual skill already possessed. One looks forward, the other looks backward.

  2. Nursing trainees — aptitude testit measures the potential for learning new skills, because they will be trained for eighteen months after joining.

Steps 3 and 4 · Interview, and reference check

Step 3 · Employment interview

An interview is a formal, in-depth conversation conducted to evaluate the applicant's suitability for the job. The role of the interviewer is to seek information and that of the interviewee is to provide it — though today the interviewee also seeks information from the interviewer.

Step 4 · Reference and background check

Employers ask for the names, addresses and telephone numbers of references in order to verify the information supplied by the applicant and gain additional information about him. Previous employers, known persons, teachers and university professors can act as references.

Example · Awadh Fresh Supermarkets, Lucknow

A candidate for cash-office supervisor interviews well. The reference check with his previous employer reveals a cash shortage he did not mention. He is eliminated at step 4 — which is precisely what step 4 is for.

Why the interview is not the whole of selection

Students treat "selection" and "interview" as synonyms. The interview is one step of eight, and it is the third. A firm that hires on the interview alone has skipped the verification (step 4) and the fitness check (step 6).

Steps 5 to 8 · Decision, medical, offer, contract

  1. Selection decisionthe final decision from among the candidates who have passed the tests, interviews and reference checks. The views of the concerned manager are generally given weight, because he is responsible for the performance of the new employee.
  2. Medical examinationafter the selection decision and before the job offer, the candidate undergoes a medical fitness test. The job offer is given only to the candidate declared fit.
  3. Job offermade to the applicant who has cleared all the previous hurdles, through a letter of appointment. It generally states a date by which the appointee must report on duty, and he must be given reasonable time for reporting.
  4. Contract of employmentafter the offer is accepted, a written contract is executed containing job title, duties and responsibilities, date of commencement, rates of pay and allowances, hours of work, leave rules, sickness, grievance procedure, disciplinary procedure, work rules and termination of employment. An attestation form is also signed, recording details authenticated by the candidate.
The order trap

Two sequence errors cost marks every year. The medical examination is step 6, not the last step — it comes after the selection decision and before the job offer. And the contract of employment comes after the offer is accepted, never before.

The confusable pair · what students actually write

Students routinely write…

"Recruitment and selection are the same thing — the company recruits the best candidate for the job." Two words are wrong in that sentence. Recruitment does not choose anybody; it only invites. And "the best candidate" is the output of selection, which is a different process, done later, with a different purpose.

One sentence that fixes it permanently

Recruitment fills the room. Selection empties it, leaving one person. Recruitment is positive and widens the pool; selection is negative and narrows it. Recruitment always precedes selection.

Numbers make it obvious

Dihing Valley put up notices and ran a radio announcement, and 1,140 people came — the pool grew from 0 to 1,140: recruitment. Then 300 were sent away at the gate as under age (1,140 → 840), the coarse-leaf test removed more, and 300 were finally taken on: the pool shrank at every stage — selection.

Distinguish · Recruitment and Selection

Four bases, each one named. Two paragraphs without a basis column lose most of the marks even when the content is right.
Basis Recruitment Selection
Meaning Searching for prospective employees and stimulating them to apply for jobs. Identifying and choosing the best person out of the prospective candidates.
Nature A positive process — it attracts and enlarges the pool. A negative process — it rejects and narrows the pool.
Objective To create a large pool of applicants. To eliminate the unsuitable and find the right person.
Sequence Comes first — step 2 of the staffing process. Comes after recruitment — step 3, and only from the pool recruitment built.
The fifth basis, if a 5-mark question asks for it

Cost and complexity. Recruitment is simple and inexpensive — an advertisement or a notice at the gate. Selection is complex, time-consuming and costly, because it is a series of tests, interviews and verification checks run on every survivor.

Training, development, education

Training

Training is any process by which the aptitudes, skills and abilities of employees to perform specific jobs are increased. It is job-oriented and short-term, and it aims to improve performance on the current job or to prepare the employee for an intended job.

Development

Development refers to the learning opportunities designed to help employees grow. It covers not only activities that improve job performance but also those that bring about growth of the personality and help the individual progress towards maturity and actualisation of his potential capacities. It is career-oriented and ongoing.

Education — the third term

Education is the process of increasing the knowledge and understanding of employees. It does not provide definite answers; it develops a logical, rational mind and the capacities of analysis, synthesis and objectivity. Education is broader in scope than training; training is tied to the goals of the organisation more than to those of the individual.

The relationship in one line

Development includes training — never the other way round. Training makes a person better at this job; development makes a person capable of the next one.

Benefits of training and development · to the organisation

A standard 5-mark question, and the answer has five headings.

  1. Systematic learningtraining is systematic learning, always better than the hit-and-trial method, which wastes effort and money.
  2. Higher productivity and profitsit enhances employee productivity both in quantity and in quality, which leads to higher profits.
  3. Equips the future managerit prepares people who can take over in an emergency — the succession question again.
  4. Higher morale, lower turnoverit increases employee morale and reduces absenteeism and employee turnover.
  5. Effective response to a changing environmentit helps the firm respond to fast technological and economic change.
Back to Chapter 3

That fifth benefit is Chapter 3 arriving inside Chapter 6: the business environment changes, and training is one of the two ways a firm absorbs the change — the other being to hire the skill from outside.

Benefits of training and development · to the employee

  1. Better careerimproved skills and knowledge lead to a better career for the individual.
  2. Higher earningsincreased performance helps the individual earn more.
  3. Fewer accidentstraining makes the employee more efficient in handling machines, and so less prone to accidents.
  4. Higher satisfaction and moralea person who can do the job well is more satisfied by it.
Case 14 · Dihing Valley Tea Estate, Guwahati

After the 2025 injuries, Bikram Saikia ran a three-week plucking-and-machine-safety programme for 400 workers at a cost of ₹9 lakh. The following season the fine leaf count returned to 66%, machine injuries fell to zero, and 62 workers qualified for the higher plucking rate of ₹8 a kilo.

Read the sentence twice: the first two results are benefits to the organisation, the third is a benefit to the employee. One case, both lists.

Distinguish · Training and Development

Students routinely write…

"Development is just long-term training." That collapses the two into one and loses the distinction the examiner is testing. Training raises knowledge and skills for the present job; development is learning and growth for the whole career and personality.

The four bases CBSE uses. Learn the basis names, not just the cells.
Basis Training Development
Meaning A process of increasing knowledge and skills to perform a specific job. A process of learning and growth of the individual in all respects.
Scope Narrow — it is short-term and confined to a job. Broad — it is an ongoing process and includes training.
Purpose To enable the employee to do the job better — a job-oriented process. To enable the overall growth of the employee — a career-oriented process.
Who it is chiefly for Chiefly for operative and technical staff learning a defined task. Chiefly for managerial and professional staff being grown for larger roles.

The methods of training

How to read this
  1. Read downwards: the amber node divides once into two families of methods.
  2. Green (left) = at the workplace, while working. Violet (right) = away from the workplace, before working.
  3. The rose box is the classification trap: vestibule training uses the real machines yet is off-the-job.
  4. Learn each list in this order — a case question asks you to name the method.

On-the-job methods · learning while doing

  1. Apprenticeship programmesthe trainee is put under the guidance of a master worker for a prescribed period of time, to acquire a higher level of skill. People entering skilled trades — plumbers, electricians, iron-workers — are often required to undergo it. Fast and slow learners are placed together, so slow learners may need additional training.
  2. Coachingthe superior guides and instructs the trainee as a coach: mutually agreed goals, suggestions on how to reach them, periodic review of progress. Classically the trainee is being groomed to replace the senior manager and to relieve him of some duties.
  3. Internship traininga joint programme of an educational institution and a business firm. Selected candidates carry on their regular studies for the prescribed period and also work in a factory or office to acquire practical knowledge and skills.
  4. Job rotationshifting the trainee from one department to another or from one job to another, so he gains a broader understanding of all parts of the business and can test his own aptitude and ability. It makes promotions, replacements and transfers easier.
Example · Awadh Fresh Supermarkets, Lucknow

Its twelve management trainees spend eight weeks each in receiving, cold storage, the checkout line, procurement and the back office before being posted. That is job rotation — and it is why the group can move a manager between stores at a week's notice.

Off-the-job methods · learning before doing

  1. Classroom lectures and conferenceswell adapted to conveying specific information — rules, procedures or methods. Audio-visuals and demonstrations make the presentation more interesting and improve retention.
  2. Filmsthey provide information and explicitly demonstrate skills not easily represented by other techniques; most effective when used with conference discussion.
  3. Case studyreal problems taken from the actual experience of organisations. Trainees determine the problem, analyse causes, develop alternatives, select the best and implement it.
  4. Computer modellingit simulates the work environment by programming a computer to imitate the realities of the job, so learning takes place without the risk or the high cost of a real mistake.
  5. Vestibule trainingemployees learn their jobs on the equipment they will actually be using, but away from the actual work floor. An actual work environment is created in a classroom with the same materials, files and equipment. Used where employees must handle sophisticated machinery and equipment.
  6. Programmed instructioninformation is broken into meaningful units arranged from simple to complex, and the trainee works through them by answering questions or filling blanks.

Where the textbook and the syllabus disagree — read this once

Both answers are then safe.

Model

★ Challenge 4 marks

A student wrote: "All six days are induction training." Name what is actually happening in each phase, classify each as on-the-job or off-the-job where the term applies, and explain why the student's answer is wrong.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Phase 1 is induction / orientationintroducing the selected employee to the other employees and familiarising him with the rules and policies of the organisation. It belongs to placement and orientation, step 4 of staffing — it is not a method of training at all, so the on-the-job / off-the-job classification does not apply to it.
  2. Phase 2 is vestibule training — OFF-the-jobthe recruits learn on the equipment they will actually be using, in a work environment created away from the actual work floor. This is the classification trap: a real CTC roller, a real drier panel, and still off-the-job.
  3. Phase 3 is coaching — ON-the-jobthe superior guides and instructs the trainee as a coach, with agreed goals, observation and a periodic review of progress, on the live shift — learning while doing.
  4. Why the student is wronginduction is only the first morning, and it increases no skill — it familiarises. Phases 2 and 3 both increase skill, which is what makes them training, and they differ from each other on the single test that matters: where the person is standing.
"a spare CTC roller and a drier control panel, identical to those on the factory floor, were installed in the disused godown behind the office, and each recruit worked them there on scrap leaf."

Exam practice · 3 marks

Question 11 3 marks

Why are internal sources of recruitment considered to be more economical?

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. No advertising or processing costthe vacancy need not be advertised and hundreds of outside applications need not be processed.
  2. Selection and placement are simplifiedthe candidate is already known and can be evaluated more accurately and economically from his record.
  3. No induction training is neededa person recruited from within already knows the rules, policies and people, so the orientation cost is saved.

Exam practice · 4 marks

Question 12 4 marks

"Staffing is a function which all managers need to perform, and it is also a separate and specialised function." Explain the statement.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Staffing is a generic function of managementlike planning, organising, directing and controlling, every manager performs it — placing the right person on the right job, orienting, training and appraising his own team.
  2. It is also a distinct functional areajust as marketing management and financial management are, and in a large firm it is carried out by a separate human resource department staffed with specialists.
  3. Hence it is both a line and a staff activitya line duty of every manager and the staff — that is, advisory — role of the HR department.
  4. Why the specialisation arisesas the organisation grows and the number of persons employed increases, managing people needs expertise — recruitment, job analysis, compensation plans, labour relations, grievances, welfare and legal defence.

Exam practice · 6 marks

Question 13 6 marks

Explain the procedure for the selection of employees.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer

6 marks → 6 points — but this question has eight steps. Write all eight headings and explain them in one line each; the examiner marks the sequence as much as the content.

  1. Preliminary screeningeliminates unqualified or unfit job-seekers on the basis of the application form; preliminary interviews reject misfits.
  2. Selection testsintelligence, aptitude, personality, trade and interest tests measure the characteristics the job needs.
  3. Employment interviewa formal, in-depth conversation to evaluate the applicant's suitability.
  4. Reference and background checkverifies the information supplied and gains additional information from previous employers and teachers.
  5. Selection decisionthe final choice among those who passed; the views of the concerned manager weigh most, since he is responsible for the new employee's performance.
  6. Medical examinationa fitness test after the decision and before the offer; the offer goes only to the candidate declared fit.
  7. Job offermade through a letter of appointment stating the date by which the appointee must report.
  8. Contract of employmenta written document of the terms — job title, duties, pay, hours, leave rules, probation and termination — signed after the offer is accepted.

Full case study A · the new plant

Case 18 · Sanchit Speciality Chemicals, Vadodara · 2026

Sanchit Speciality Chemicals is setting up a new plant to make auto coatings, aiming at 40% of the Indian market and ₹50 crore of exports within two years. It needs a highly trained and motivated workforce, and it has no operations in that product line today. Its Managing Director, Kirit Shah, has said he wants "the plant staffed by the time the machinery is commissioned in nine months, with people who can run equipment they have never seen".

Question 14 6 marks

(a) Outline the process of staffing the company should follow. (b) Which sources of recruitment should it rely on, and why? (c) Which training method should it use for the operators, and why?

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. (a) Start with estimating manpower requirementsworkload analysis against workforce analysis, translated into job descriptions and candidate profiles — the plant is new, so almost everything is a shortage.
  2. (a) Then recruitment, selection, placement and orientationbuild the pool, narrow it through the eight-step funnel, put each person in his post and familiarise him with the rules and the people.
  3. (a) Then training and development, appraisal, promotion, compensationthe last four steps are what convert a filled post into a performing post.
  4. (b) Mainly external sourcesa new product line means the skill does not exist inside, so external sources give qualified personnel, wider choice and fresh talent — campus recruitment for engineers, placement agencies and management consultants for senior technical posts, advertisement for the rest.
  5. (b) With internal sources for the transferable rolessafety, maintenance, accounts and stores can be filled by transfer and promotion, which is cheap, quick and carries the firm's culture to the new plant.
  6. (c) Vestibule training for the operatorsthey must handle sophisticated machinery they have never seen; a replica set-up away from the actual work floor lets them learn without risking the plant or delaying commissioning.
"the plant staffed by the time the machinery is commissioned in nine months, with people who can run equipment they have never seen".

Full case study B · the new HR manager

Case 19 · Dihing Valley Tea Estate, Guwahati · 2026

Ms Jayshree Barua has just been appointed the estate's first Human Resource Manager. Dihing Valley employs 800 people across its gardens and factory, and Bikram Saikia's expansion into the adjoining Namrup block will need 200 more. In her first week she finds that no job descriptions exist, that vacancies are filled by whoever the field managers happen to know, that nobody has been appraised for three years, and that the last four factory supervisors all resigned within a year of joining.

Question 15 6 marks

(a) What functions is she supposed to perform? (b) What problems do you foresee in her job? (c) What steps should she take?

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. (a) The specialised duties of HRMrecruitment, analysing jobs and preparing job descriptions, developing compensation and incentive plans, training and development, labour and union–management relations, handling grievances, social security and welfare, and defending the company in law suits.
  2. (b) Problem — no manpower estimatewith no job descriptions there is no basis for workload and workforce analysis, so 200 additional people cannot be planned for.
  3. (b) Problem — recruitment by acquaintancerelying on whom the field managers know narrows the pool instead of widening it, which defeats the positive purpose of recruitment.
  4. (b) Problem — no appraisal, and heavy turnoverwithout performance appraisal there is no basis for promotion, training needs or compensation; four supervisors resigning in a year points to weak selection and to absent orientation.
  5. (c) Step — build the baseprepare job descriptions and candidate profiles, then run the staffing process in order, using external sources for the specialised expansion posts and internal transfer and promotion for the rest.
  6. (c) Step — install the missing machinerya structured selection process, an induction programme for every joiner, a training plan, and an annual appraisal with feedback against predetermined standards.
