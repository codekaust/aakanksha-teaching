Chapter 3
Business Environment

A manager can control the people inside the gate. Nothing outside it. This chapter is about the forces the firm cannot command but must not ignore — and about how a manager reads them before they arrive.

Where we were

Chapters 1 and 2 → Chapter 3

Chapters 1 and 2 looked inside the firm. Chapter 1 asked what management is — a process that gets things done through others, effectively and efficiently. Chapter 2 asked what guides it — Fayol's fourteen principles and Taylor's scientific management.

This chapter turns the camera round. The same manager, the same principles — but now the world outside the gate is moving. Interest rates change, a competitor opens next door, a law is notified, a technology makes yesterday's product unsellable.

The link that matters

Fayol's principles tell a manager how to act. They do not tell her what is coming. A principle of management is a general guideline; the environment is the situation in which that guideline has to be applied — and Chapter 2 already told you that principles are contingent, i.e. their application depends on the situation. Chapter 3 is that situation.

What "business environment" means

Definition — learn this wording

Business environment means the sum total of all individuals, institutions and other forces that are outside a business enterprise but that potentially affect its performance.

The one-line version worth memorising

"Take the universe, subtract from it the subset that represents the organisation, and the remainder is the environment."

The definition has two halves and both are marked

Half one: outside the enterprise. Half two: affects its performance. A student who writes only "everything outside the business" has written half a definition. The moon is outside the business; it is not part of its environment.

"Outside the enterprise" or "outside the control of the enterprise"?

Both phrasings appear in the NCERT chapter, and they are not identical. The body text reads: "the sum total of all individuals, institutions and other forces that are outside the control of a business enterprise but that may affect its performance." The chapter summary reads: "the totality of all individuals, institutions and other forces that are outside a business but that potentially affect its performance."

Write the summary version, with "enterprise" restored: "the sum total of all individuals, institutions and other forces that are outside a business enterprise but that potentially affect its performance." "Sum total" and "totality" are both accepted; what you must not drop is "outside" and "affect its performance". Avoid "beyond the control of" — it is also the less defensible statement.

Why more defensible: a large firm is not entirely powerless over its environment. A lobbying campaign can shape a law; an advertising campaign can shift a social trend; a patent can change the technological environment for a whole industry. So "beyond control" overstates it. What is always true is that these forces are external to the firm and affect its performance.

If a question specifically asks "why can a firm not control its environment?", answer with relativity, complexity and uncertainty — not by quoting the definition.

The environment is not a background — it is a force

Case 1 · Godavari Life Sciences, Nashik

Dr Sneha Kulkarni runs a 240-worker unit at Nashik that formulates generic medicines and exports about 60 per cent of its output. In one quarter of 2023 three things happened that she had not planned for: the rupee weakened against the dollar, a competitor in Hyderabad launched the same molecule at a lower price, and the regulator revised the labelling norms for exported cartons. Not one of these three decisions was taken inside her factory, yet all three changed her costs and her order book within the same quarter.

Question 1 3 marks

Using the case, explain what is meant by business environment. Quote the line that shows why these forces belong to the environment rather than to the firm.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer · 3 marks → 3 points

  2. Outside the enterpriseThe exchange rate, the competitor's pricing and the labelling norm were all settled outside Godavari's gate — by the currency market, by a rival firm and by a regulator.
  3. Yet they affect performanceThey moved her costs and her order book in a single quarter, which is precisely the second half of the definition.

Sizing the answer — the rule for the whole subject

Points ≈ marks. 3 marks → 3 points · 4 marks → 4 · 5–6 marks → 5 or 6. Every point is a bolded heading followed by one or two lines. A heading on its own earns half the mark; an unheaded paragraph earns less than either. A Business Studies answer is never a paragraph.

The five-step answer — drill it from today

How a case-study answer is built
  1. Read the case twice. The second reading is for the sentence that carries the clue, not for the story.
  2. Identify the concept. Which feature, dimension or benefit is this?
  3. Name it in the exam's exact words. "Technological environment", not "new machines".
  4. Explain it in one line. The textbook definition, compressed.
  5. Quote the line from the case that proves it. Begin with "This is evident from the line…".
Where the marks are actually lost

Steps 3 and 5. Students who write a correct explanation but never name the concept lose the identification mark; students who name it but never quote the case lose the application mark. 60% of this paper is application and analysis — those two marks are the difference between a 70 and a 90.

Seven features — the first three

1 · Totality of external forces

The environment is the aggregate of everything external to the firm — it is not one force, it is the sum.

2024: a Nashik exporter's environment is the rupee, the regulator, the shipping line and the Hyderabad rival — all at once.

2 · Specific and general forces

Specific forces — investors, customers, competitors, suppliers — affect one enterprise directly and immediately. General forces — social, political, legal, technological — affect all firms and so affect one firm only indirectly.

3 · Inter-relatedness

The parts of the environment are closely inter-related — a change in one shows up in another.

NCERT's example: rising life expectancy and health awareness (social) raised demand for fat-free oils and health resorts (economic), which in turn changed lifestyles (social again).

Reading inter-relatedness in one sentence

If a case sentence contains the word "which led to", "as a result" or "this in turn" and the two halves belong to different dimensions, the examiner is testing inter-relatedness. Nothing else looks like that.

Seven features — the last four

4 · Dynamic nature

The environment keeps changing — technological improvement, shifts in consumer preference, entry of new competition.

2016 → 2024: UPI went from a launch (2016) to the default way an Indian shop is paid. The fact of change is dynamism.

5 · Uncertainty

The environment is largely uncertain because future happenings are very difficult to predict, especially where change is fast — information technology, fashion.

Dynamism is that it will change; uncertainty is that you cannot say how.

6 · Complexity

It consists of numerous inter-related and dynamic forces from different sources, so it is easier to understand in parts than in totality.

You cannot say how much of a fall in sales was social, how much economic and how much technological. That inability is complexity.

7 · Relativity

The environment is a relative concept — it differs from country to country and even region to region.

Demand for sarees is high in India and nearly non-existent in France; political conditions in the USA differ from those in China.

Hook — seven features, in order Totality · Specific & general · Inter-related · Dynamic · Uncertain · Complex · Relative

The hook gets you the seven headings. It does not get you the marks — each heading still needs its one line of explanation and, in an application question, its example.

Case · which feature is this?

Case 2 · Chandpole Handicrafts Co-operative, Jaipur

Kailash Meena heads a 90-artisan block-printing co-operative in Jaipur. Between 2019 and 2024 his order pattern changed three times — first a collapse in tourist buying, then a surge in online export orders, then a shift towards organic-dye fabrics. "Every time we retooled for one trend," he says, "the next one had already started, and none of us could have told you in advance which way it would go."

Question 2 4 marks

Identify and explain the two features of business environment illustrated in the case. Support each with the line from the case.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer · 4 marks → 2 features × (name + explain)
  1. Dynamic natureThe business environment keeps on changing — in tastes, technology and competition. Kailash's order pattern changed three times in five years (2019–2024).
  2. UncertaintyFuture happenings are very difficult to predict. He could not say in advance which trend would come next.
  3. The words that force the reading"the next one had already started" gives you dynamic nature; "none of us could have told you in advance" gives you uncertainty.
  4. Why not complexityComplexity would need him to say he could not tell which force was responsible. He knows exactly which forces they were — he just could not time them.
"Every time we retooled for one trend, the next one had already started, and none of us could have told you in advance which way it would go."

★ Challenge · dynamism, uncertainty or complexity?

★ Challenge 4 marks

A student writes: "Uncertainty and dynamic nature are the same feature written twice — if the environment changes, obviously you cannot predict it." Is the student right? Answer with a case of your own that separates the two.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. The student is wrong, but for a subtle reasonChange and unpredictability are different claims. A change can be perfectly predictable and still be a change.
  2. Dynamic but certainThe GST rate on a product is revised on a notified date, published weeks in advance (GST has run since 1 July 2017). The environment has changed — dynamism — but nobody was surprised. So there is dynamism without uncertainty.
  3. Uncertain even about the directionWhether Indian consumers will accept a new food category is unknowable until it is tried. Here the uncertainty is the whole problem.
  4. The examiner's distinctionDynamic nature = it will change. Uncertainty = you cannot say how or when. Write both sentences and the mark is safe.

Why a manager must understand the environment

A firm cannot change these forces. It has no alternative but to respond and adapt — and it can only respond to what it has seen.

1 · Opportunities and the first mover advantage

Opportunities are positive external trends that will improve performance. Spotting them early lets a firm exploit them before a competitor does.

NCERT's own example: Maruti Udyog led the small-car market because it was first to read rising petrol prices plus a large middle class.

2 · Threats and early warning signals

Threats are external trends that will hinder performance. Awareness gives the manager time to improve quality, cut cost or advertise harder.

A foreign multinational announcing entry with a substitute product is a warning signal, not yet a loss.

3 · Tapping useful resources

The environment supplies the firm's inputs — finance, machines, raw material, power, labour — and absorbs its outputs. Knowing what the environment has to offer is how a firm gets those inputs on good terms.

2023: the Jaipur co-operative found a natural-dye supplier in Sanganer only because it was watching the organic-textile trade, not its own order book.

4 · Coping with rapid change

It is not the fact of change but the pace of change that matters. Fragmented markets, less brand loyalty, more demanding customers — a manager must examine the environment to develop a suitable course of action.

2024: a Chennai dealership took on an electric brand and retrained four mechanics as petrol-scooter sales fell for a third year.

5 · Assisting in planning and policy formulation

The environment is the source of both opportunities and threats, so its analysis is the basis for deciding the future course of action (planning) and the guidelines for decision making (policy).

6 · Improving performance

Firms that continuously monitor their environment and adapt their practices do not merely survive — studies find they perform better and succeed for longer.

2016 → 2024: the Indore start-up that read UPI early still holds the city; the rival that entered in 2023 is still buying share.

Hook — six benefits, in NCERT's order Opportunity · Threat · Resources · Change · Planning · Performance

Benefit 5 is the bridge to Chapter 4

Chapter 3 → Chapter 4

"Assisting in planning and policy formulation" is not just one item on a list of six. It is the handover. Environmental scanning produces the assumptions about the future on which every plan rests — Chapter 4 will call these the premises, and developing them is step 2 of the planning process.

So the honest sentence is: a plan is only as good as the environmental scan behind it. A firm that reads the environment badly does not make a bad decision — it makes a decision from bad premises, which is worse, because everything downstream inherits the error.

Say this in the paper

When a 6-mark question asks for the importance of business environment, close your answer with one line linking it forward: "Since the environment supplies the premises on which plans are built, its analysis is the starting point of the planning function." It is true, it is examinable, and almost nobody writes it.

Case · the first mover advantage

Case 3 · Malwa Minutes, Indore

Ritika Bhandari founded a quick-commerce grocery start-up in Indore in 2021. Watching UPI volumes and smartphone ownership in tier-two cities, she concluded that Indore households would order ₹300 baskets for ten-minute delivery two years before any national player thought a tier-two city was worth a dark store. By the time the first national competitor opened in Indore in 2023, Malwa Minutes already had eleven dark stores and 40 per cent of the city's online grocery orders.

Question 3 4 marks

Identify and explain the benefit of understanding the business environment that Ritika obtained. State the two environmental changes she read, and name their dimensions.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer · 4 marks → 4 points
  1. Identification of opportunities and first mover advantageEarly identification of a positive external trend lets an enterprise be the first to exploit it instead of losing it to competitors.
  2. The change she read — UPI volumesA technological change: digital payment made a ₹300 basket collectable without cash handling.
  3. The change she read — rising tier-two incomes and smartphone ownershipAn economic change: rising disposable income creates demand.
  4. The advantage realisedTwo years of unopposed trading gave her scale and share before rivals arrived — the definition of the first mover advantage.
By the time the first national competitor opened in Indore in 2023, Malwa Minutes already had eleven dark stores and 40 per cent of the city's online grocery orders.

Opportunity or threat? — the same fact, two firms

For Malwa Minutes, Indore — an opportunity

The 2023 arrival of a national quick-commerce chain in Indore expanded the number of households willing to order groceries online at all. A larger category, and Ritika already had the dark stores.

For a Chennai two-wheeler dealership — a threat

The same rise in ten-minute delivery cut the number of short errand trips a household makes, and errand trips are what sells a scooter. Nothing changed inside the showroom; the environment changed around it.

The examinable point

An environmental change is not intrinsically an opportunity or a threat. It becomes one or the other relative to the firm — which is the feature of relativity doing its work. In a case question, always say for whom it is an opportunity.

Feature 2, opened up: specific and general forces

Definition — specific forces

Specific forces — such as investors, customers, competitors and suppliers — are those which affect an individual enterprise directly and immediately in its day-to-day working.

Definition — general forces

General forces — such as social, political, legal and technological conditions — have an impact on all business enterprises and thus may affect an individual firm only indirectly.

Two sentences that decide every question on this pair

Specific: one firm, directly, today.  ·  General: all firms, indirectly, through the conditions they all trade in.

The examiner's favourite trap on this pair

The five dimensions you are about to learn — economic, social, technological, political, legal — are all general forces. Customers, suppliers, competitors and investors are specific. A student who lists "economic environment" as a specific force has confused the two halves of the chapter with each other.

The two rings around the firm

How to read this
  1. Start at the amber centre — that is the firm, and it is the only thing here that is not environment.
  2. Solid blue arrows from the inner ring: these forces touch this firm directly and today.
  3. Dashed violet arrows from the outer ring: these reach the firm only after passing through the inner ring — that is what "indirect" means.
  4. The test for which ring: ask "does this force know my firm's name?" A customer does. Inflation does not.

The test, applied

The force in the caseRingWhy — the test
A rival opens a showroom 400 m away in 2024Specific It competes for this firm's walk-in customers, from the day it opens.
The RBI raises the repo rateGeneral It raises borrowing costs for every firm; this firm feels it only through its own loan.
A supplier of packaging doubles his priceSpecific A named supplier, a named contract, an immediate cost increase.

 Six drills. If you can do these six cold, this pair cannot cost you a mark.
One sentence to carry into the exam

A general force becomes visible to a firm only after it has changed the behaviour of a specific force — inflation reaches you through your supplier's invoice, a social trend reaches you through your customer's order. That is exactly what the dashed arrows in the diagram were showing.

Confusable pair · specific vs general environment

Written in the words students actually use

"Specific forces are the small ones and general forces are the big ones."

"Customers are general, because every business has customers."

Both are wrong, and the second one is the expensive one. The division is not by size and not by how common the force is. It is by whom the force acts on: a force that acts on this enterprise by name is specific; a force that acts on all enterprises alike is general. Your customers are specific precisely because they are your customers.

Three bases is the right length for a 3-mark question; add "Examples" for 4.

Case · a case that can only be read one way

Case 4 · Marina Motors, Chennai

R. Sundaram's two-wheeler dealership had a difficult 2023-24. Two things hit him. First, the manufacturer he represents cut his dealer margin from 4.5 per cent to 3.8 per cent from April 2023. Second, road-tax rates on petrol two-wheelers were revised upward by the State in the same year. "The margin cut was a letter addressed to me by name; the tax change was in the newspaper and applied to every dealer in Tamil Nadu."

Question 4 3 marks

Classify each of the two forces as specific or general, giving your reason. State the test you applied.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer · 3 marks → 3 points
  1. The margin cut — a specific forceThe manufacturer is a supplier to the dealership, and the cut affected this enterprise directly and immediately in its day-to-day working.
  2. The road-tax revision — a general forceA change in the legal environment binding on all dealers in the State alike, therefore affecting this firm only indirectly — through its customers' willingness to buy.
  3. The testDoes the force deal with this firm by name? If yes, specific; if it applies to everyone, general.
"The margin cut was a letter addressed to me by name; the tax change was in the newspaper and applied to every dealer in Tamil Nadu."

Notice the case was built so that only one reading survives. That is what a good case does — and it is what you must do when the reverse drill asks you to write one.

★ Challenge · the force that changes ring

★ Challenge 4 marks

In 2024 the Government notified a new quality-control order requiring every pharmaceutical exporter to install serialised track-and-trace printing. For most exporters this was one more compliance cost. For Godavari Life Sciences, Nashik, it was different: Godavari's only supplier of that printing equipment then quoted a delivery date beyond the compliance deadline.

Is the track-and-trace order a specific force or a general force for Godavari? Defend your answer — and explain what feature of business environment this case is really about.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. The order itself is a general forceIt is a legal requirement applying to all pharmaceutical exporters alike. That it is expensive does not make it specific — cost is not the test.
  2. Its effect arrived as a specific forceWhat actually hurt Godavari was its supplier's delivery date — a named supplier, dealing with this firm, in its day-to-day working. That is specific.
  3. The feature being testedInter-relatedness — a change in one part of the environment (legal) produced a change in another (a specific supplier relationship).

The five dimensions of business environment

Definition

The dimensions of business environment are the factors constituting it — economic, social, technological, political and legal conditions — which are considered relevant for decision making and improving the performance of an enterprise.

Before the diagram — get this straight

These five are the general environment, not the specific one. NCERT says so in a single sentence that students read past: "In contrast to the specific environment, these factors explain the general environment which mostly influences many enterprises at the same time."

Hook — five dimensions Economic · Social · Technological · Political · Legal  →  "Every Shop Trades Past Lunch"

The firm at the centre of five forces

Dimension 1 · Economic environment

Definition

The economic environment consists of forces such as interest rates, inflation rates, changes in the disposable income of people, stock market indices and the value of the rupee, which affect a firm's demand and its costs.

NCERT's own worked example

Low long-term interest rates benefit construction companies and automobile manufacturers, because consumers spend more on homes and cars bought on borrowed money.

Conversely, high inflation constrains a firm by raising the cost of raw material, machinery, wages and salaries.

Case 5 · Marina Motors, Chennai

Through 2022-23 lending rates on two-wheeler finance rose with the RBI's repo rate, and R. Sundaram watched the effect arrive at his counter. Showroom footfall held steady but conversions fell: buyers walked in, saw the revised EMI and postponed the purchase.

Question 5 3 marks

Name the dimension at work, explain it, and quote the proving line.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Economic environmentIt consists of forces such as interest rates, inflation and disposable income which affect a firm's demand and costs.
  2. How it operated hereA rise in interest rates raised the EMI, which reduced the customer's willingness to buy on borrowed money.
  3. Why not social or technologicalNothing changed in what buyers wanted or in how scooters are made — only in what the purchase cost per month. The clue word is EMI.
Showroom footfall held steady but conversions fell: buyers walked in, saw the revised EMI and postponed the purchase.

Dimension 2 · Social environment

Definition

The social environment consists of social forces such as customs and traditions, values, social trends, literacy rate, life expectancy, consumption habits, composition of the family and society's expectations from business.

Traditions

Practices lasting decades or centuries. Diwali, Eid, Christmas and Guru Parv create demand for greeting cards, sweets and tailoring every year.

Values

What society holds in high esteem — individual freedom, social justice, equality of opportunity. In business terms: freedom of choice, responsibility towards society, non-discriminatory employment.

Social trends

The health-and-fitness trend among urban dwellers created demand for organic food, gyms, bottled water and food supplements.

Case 6 · Chandpole Handicrafts Co-operative, Jaipur

Between 2019 and 2024 Kailash Meena's export buyers began asking for certificates of natural dye and fair wages before placing an order. "They no longer only ask what the fabric costs — they ask what we pay the woman who printed it, and whether the dye went into the Dravyavati river."

Question 6 3 marks

Identify the dimension, name the specific element of it, and quote the line.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Social environmentIt includes society's expectations from business and the values a society holds in high esteem.
  2. The elementSociety's expectations from business — buyers now expect fair wages and environmental responsibility as a condition of trade, not as a favour.
  3. Why not legalNo law compelled the certificates; the buyers did. When compliance is demanded by society rather than by statute, it is social, not legal.
"They no longer only ask what the fabric costs — they ask what we pay the woman who printed it, and whether the dye went into the Dravyavati river."

Dimension 3 · Technological environment

Definition

The technological environment includes forces relating to scientific improvements and innovations which provide new ways of producing goods and services and new methods and techniques of operating a business.

The shifts NCERT lists — all of them historical

Vacuum tubes → transistors · steam locomotives → diesel and electric · fountain pens → ballpoint · propeller aircraft → jets · typewriters → word processors. Each one created one industry and destroyed another.

The Indian shifts your class has actually lived through

2016: UPI launched — cash counters give way to QR codes. 2020-21: video consultation becomes normal medical practice. 2023-24: ten-minute delivery reaches tier-two cities.

Case 7 · Malwa Minutes, Indore

Ritika Bhandari's entire business model rests on one fact. Before UPI, a ₹300 grocery order to a stranger's door had to be settled in cash, which made ten-minute delivery unprofitable to collect; after 2016 the payment was already done before the rider left the store.

Question 7 3 marks

Which dimension made Malwa Minutes possible, and why is the answer not "economic"?

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer
  1. Technological environmentIt supplies new methods and techniques of operating a business — here, a new way to collect payment.
  2. Why not economicThe invention of a cheaper method is technological; the fall in cost that follows is economic. The case is about the method existing at all, so the answer is technological.
  3. The transferable ruleAsk what changed: the way work is done (technological) or the money involved (economic)?
Before UPI, a ₹300 grocery order to a stranger's door had to be settled in cash, which made ten-minute delivery unprofitable to collect; after 2016 the payment was already done before the rider left the store.

Dimension 4 · Political environment

Definition

The political environment includes political conditions such as the general stability and peace in the country and the specific attitudes that elected government representatives hold towards business.

Why stability is the whole idea

The significance of political conditions lies in the predictability of business activity. Political stability builds confidence to invest in long-term projects; political instability shakes that confidence. Notice what this means: the political environment is examined mostly through its effect on long-horizon decisions.

Case 8 · Godavari Life Sciences, Nashik

Dr Sneha Kulkarni was weighing a ₹42 crore second formulation block with a payback period of eleven years. "I am not committing eleven years of capital to a country whose attitude to pharmaceutical exports might reverse at the next election," she told her board — and asked instead for a note on how consistently that policy had been held over the previous decade.

Question 8 4 marks

Identify the dimension and explain, in four points, why it matters more to this decision than to a decision about next month's raw-material purchase.

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer · 4 marks → 4 points
  1. Political environmentGeneral stability and peace in the country and the attitude of elected representatives towards business.
  2. It works through predictabilityStable political conditions make business activity predictable; instability makes it unpredictable.
  3. Which decisions it bindsAn eleven-year payback commits capital across several governments, so it needs political stability. A one-month raw-material purchase does not.
  4. The proving behaviourShe asked for the consistency of the policy over a decade — she was measuring stability, which is precisely the political dimension.
"I am not committing eleven years of capital to a country whose attitude to pharmaceutical exports might reverse at the next election."

Dimension 5 · Legal environment

Definition

The legal environment includes the various legislations passed by the Government, the administrative orders issued by government authorities, court judgments, and the decisions of the various commissions and agencies at every level of government — centre, state or local.

NCERT's illustrations — consumer protection through advertising rules

Advertisement of alcoholic beverages is prohibited · cigarette packets must carry the statutory warning · advertisements for baby food must state that mother's milk is best. Non-compliance lands the enterprise in legal trouble, so adequate knowledge of the rules is a pre-requisite for better performance.

Where the textbook is now out of date — say the right one in the paper

NCERT's list of Indian legislation includes the Consumer Protection Act, 1986. That Act has been repealed and replaced by the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, which is what Chapter 12 of this course teaches — District, State and National Commissions, the CCPA, product liability, e-filing and mediation. Political or legal? — the boundary that costs marks

Written in the words students actually use

"A new law was passed, so it is political — the government did it."

The government is involved in both, so "the government did it" decides nothing. The division is between who governs and how stable they are (political) and the rule that is actually on the statute book (legal).

Three bases. In a case, the word "must" almost always signals legal.
BasisPolitical environmentLegal environment
Meaning Stability and peace in the country and the attitude of elected representatives towards business. Legislations, administrative orders, court judgments and decisions of commissions and agencies.
What it produces Confidence or fear — a climate in which to commit long-term capital. Obligation — something the firm must do or must not do, with a penalty attached.
Test in a case Words like stability, unrest, election, attitude of the government. Words like Act, notification, order, prohibited, mandatory, court held.
If a law is the government acting, why are political and legal separate dimensions?

Because they act on the firm at different speeds and through different mechanisms, and a manager responds to them differently.

A legal change is a discrete obligation with a date: on 1 July 2017 GST applied, and every firm had to comply or be penalised. The manager's response is compliance, and it is measurable.

A political change is a continuous climate: it produces no obligation at all. It changes the probability that future laws will be favourable, which is why it is felt in long-horizon decisions — plant investment, foreign collaboration — and not in this week's operations. The manager's response is not compliance but the willingness to commit capital.

Useful test: can you be fined for it? If yes, it is legal. If it only makes you more or less willing to invest, it is political.

Demonetisation — the concept

Definition — with the date inside it

Demonetisation is the act of stripping a currency unit of its status as legal tender. On 8 November 2016 the Government of India demonetised the ₹500 and ₹1,000 notes with immediate effect — they ceased to be legal tender except for a few specified purposes such as paying utility bills — which rendered about 86 per cent of the money in circulation invalid.

The aim, in the exam's words

To curb corruption, counterfeiting, the use of high denomination notes for illegal activities, and especially the accumulation of black money — income that has not been declared to the tax authorities.

Two errors made on sight

Demonetisation is not devaluation. Devaluation lowers the external exchange value of a currency; demonetisation withdraws legal tender status from particular notes. The rupee's value against the dollar did not change on 8 November 2016 because of this.

It did not make India "cashless". The stated aim was a less-cash or cash-lite economy, and new ₹500 and ₹2,000 notes were issued by the RBI immediately afterwards.

The four features of demonetisation

A closed list of four, and therefore a standard 4-mark question. Learn them as four headings.

1 · A tax administration measure

Cash arising from declared income could simply be deposited and exchanged. Those holding black money had to declare unaccounted wealth and pay tax at a penalty rate.

2 · A signal that tax evasion will not be tolerated

It is interpreted as a shift on the part of the Government, indicating that tax evasion will no longer be tolerated or accepted.

3 · Channelling savings into the formal financial system

Deposits flowed into banks. Even though much of that cash would be withdrawn again, the new deposit schemes continue to provide a base for loans at lower interest rates.

4 · Creating a less-cash or cash-lite economy

More savings routed through the formal financial system and improved tax compliance — which depends on cell phones for customers and PoS machines for merchants, and therefore on connectivity.

Why this sits in a chapter about the environment

Because it is the cleanest single instance of dimensions colliding. Demonetisation was a political decision executed as a legal instrument, whose consequences were economic and — through the shift to digital payment — technological. One event, four dimensions: inter-relatedness taken from life rather than from a textbook example.

Why 1991 — and why it is taught as history

Read the tense on this slide carefully

Everything from here to the end of this section happened thirty-five years ago. It is on the syllabus not as news but as the cause of the environment your case-study firms trade in. Write it in the past tense, and say 1991 every time.

The crisis of June 1991 — the trigger, in five figures

The fiscal deficit had reached 6.6 per cent of GDP in 1990-91 · internal debt was about 50 per cent of GDP, with interest payments taking 39 per cent of central revenue · GNP growth had fallen to 1.4 per cent · inflation ran at 13–14 per cent · and foreign exchange reserves were barely enough for a few weeks of imports. In May 1991 the Government leased 20 tonnes of gold to the State Bank of India, and the RBI pledged 47 tonnes to the Bank of England to raise $600 million.

Why the story matters for the answer

The reforms were not a change of political taste. They were a response to a payments crisis the country could not otherwise settle. A student who can say that in one line has explained why the environment changed, which is worth more than listing what changed.

If 1991 is history, why is it still examined?

Because the syllabus asks a specific question: the impact of Government policy changes on business and industry. That impact — increased competition, more demanding customers, faster technological change, market orientation — is the environment your firm is in today. 1991 is the dated cause; the seven impacts are the undated present.

So the honest teaching order is: date the policy precisely (New Industrial Policy, July 1991) and write the impacts as conditions that still hold. A student who dates the impacts to 1991 has misunderstood the question — competition did not increase for one year and stop.

In the paper: "The New Industrial Policy of July 1991 liberalised, privatised and globalised the Indian economy. As a result, Indian enterprises today face…" — past tense for the policy, present tense for the impact.

The New Industrial Policy, July 1991

Six measures. Together they are the whole of liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation in one announcement.

  1. Industries under compulsory licensing were reduced to six.
  2. Many industries reserved for the public sector were dereserved; the public sector's role was limited to four industries of strategic importance.
  3. Disinvestment was carried out in many public sector enterprises.
  4. Policy towards foreign capital was liberalised — the share of foreign equity participation was raised and 100 per cent FDI was permitted in many activities.
  5. Automatic permission was granted for technology agreements with foreign companies.
  6. The Foreign Investment Promotion Board (FIPB) was set up to promote and channelise foreign investment into India.
The one-line summary the board wants

The policy sought to liberate industry from the shackles of the licensing system (liberalisation), drastically reduce the role of the public sector (privatisation), and encourage foreign private participation in India's industrial development (globalisation).

Liberalisation — freeing the firm from controls

Definition

Liberalisation means freeing Indian business and industry from all unnecessary controls and restrictions. It signalled the end of the licence–permit–quota raj.

What was liberalised — the seven

Abolishing licensing in most industries · freedom in deciding the scale of business · removal of restrictions on the movement of goods and services · freedom in fixing prices · reduction in tax rates and lifting of unnecessary controls · simplified import and export procedures · easier access to foreign capital and technology.

What it meant at the gate of a firm

Before 1991, a Nashik formulation unit wanting to double its capacity needed a licence to do it. After 1991 it needed a bank. The constraint moved from permission to finance — which is why Part B of this course exists.

Do not blur the three

Liberalisation removes controls. It does not change who owns the firm, and it does not by itself open the border. Dropping a licence requirement is liberalisation; selling a public-sector firm is privatisation; letting a German buyer place an order directly is globalisation.

Privatisation — changing who owns

Definition

Privatisation means giving a greater role to the private sector and a reduced role to the public sector in the nation-building process.

How it was carried out, from 1991

The Government redefined the role of the public sector in the New Industrial Policy, adopted a policy of planned disinvestment, and decided to refer loss-making and sick enterprises to the Board of Industrial and Financial Reconstruction.

Disinvestment — the term the examiner wants

Disinvestment means the transfer of ownership in public sector enterprises to the private sector. It dilutes the Government's stake. Where dilution goes beyond 51 per cent, both ownership and management pass to the private sector.

Two dated Indian instances — and they fall on opposite sides of the line

January 2022 · Air India. The Government sold its entire 100 per cent holding to Talace Private Limited, a Tata Sons subsidiary, and handed the airline over on 27 January 2022. Ownership and management left the public sector — privatisation in the fullest sense.

May 2022 · LIC. The Government sold about 3.5 per cent of Life Insurance Corporation through a public issue. That is disinvestment, and it is privatisation as policy — but the Government still holds far more than 51 per cent, so control did not move.

The 51 per cent line is the mark

Any disinvestment is privatisation in policy. Only disinvestment past 51 per cent transfers control. If a question gives you a percentage, it is testing exactly this — say which side of 51 the figure falls on and what follows. Air India is above the line; LIC is far below it.

Globalisation — opening the border

Definition

Globalisation means the integration of the various economies of the world, leading towards the emergence of a cohesive global economy.

How India pursued it after 1991

Until 1991 imports were regulated by licensing, tariff restrictions and quantitative restrictions. The reforms aimed at import liberalisation, export promotion through rationalising the tariff structure, and reforms in foreign exchange.

A truly globalised economy — the six freedoms

Free flow of goods and services · free flow of capital · free flow of information and technology · free movement of people · a common mechanism for settlement of disputes · a global governance perspective.

What it means for a firm

Physical distance and political boundaries are no longer barriers to serving a distant customer. A 90-artisan co-operative in Jaipur now sells to a household in Rotterdam — a transaction that in 1985 would have required an export house, a licence and a quota.

The impact on business and industry — the seven

This is the part of the section that is still present tense. These are conditions your case-study firms are living in.

1 · Increasing competition

Delicensing and foreign entry mean an Indian firm competes with domestic and multinational rivals at once.

2 · More demanding customers

Wider choice and better information make the customer the centre of the market.

3 · Rapidly changing technological environment

Competing on technology forces firms to keep re-equipping — and small firms feel it hardest.

4 · Necessity for change

Firms can no longer plan for long stable periods; they must continuously modify operations.

5 · Need for developing human resources

New skills and higher competence are needed, which is why Chapter 6 (Staffing) matters commercially and not only administratively.

6 · Market orientation

Firms shifted from a production-first approach to studying market needs first and producing accordingly.

7 · Loss of budgetary support to the public sector

Public enterprises could no longer rely on Government budgetary support and had to generate their own resources.

Where impact 6 goes next

Market orientation is the whole of Chapter 11 (Marketing) in embryo — the shift from the production concept to the marketing concept. When you meet it there, remember it started here, in 1991.

Exam practice · full case study 2

Case 15 · Marina Motors, Chennai

R. Sundaram's dealership has faced four changes since 2016: digital payment replaced cash deposits after demonetisation; lending rates rose through 2022-23; State road tax on petrol two-wheelers was revised upward in 2023; and by 2024 a visible share of his walk-in customers were asking only about electric models. "Not one of these four was decided by me, and together they have changed what I stock, how I am paid and whom I hire." He has since taken on an electric brand and retrained four mechanics.

Question 20 6 marks

(a) Identify the four dimensions at work. (b) Name the two features of business environment the case as a whole demonstrates, quoting the line. (c) Which benefit of understanding the environment did Sundaram obtain?

Are you ready for the answer? 🤔
Answer · 6 marks → 6 points
  1. Legal and politicalDemonetisation — withdrawal of legal tender status by an elected Government — and the State road-tax revision.
  2. EconomicThe rise in lending rates through 2022-23, which raised the EMI and deferred purchases.
  3. TechnologicalDigital payment replacing cash, and the shift of customer interest to electric two-wheelers.
  4. SocialCustomers asking only about electric models is a change in consumption habits and social expectation, not a change in the law.
  5. Two featuresTotality of external forces — "not one of these four was decided by me" — and dynamic nature, four changes in eight years.
  6. The benefit obtainedCoping with rapid changes: he examined the environment and developed a suitable course of action — an electric brand and retrained mechanics.

Before you leave this chapter

The five habits this chapter was really teaching
  1. Date every example1991, 8 November 2016, 1 July 2017. An undated claim about the environment is a guess about which decade you are in.
  2. Name the concept in the exam's words"Technological environment", not "new machines". "First mover advantage", not "they were early".
  3. Quote the proving lineAlways begin the last sentence of a case answer with "This is evident from the line…".
  4. Count your points against the marks1 → one line · 3 → three points · 4 → four · 6 → five or six with examples.
  5. Never let "environment" shrink to "economy"Five dimensions, two rings. Three of the four forces that decide a firm's future are usually not economic at all.
